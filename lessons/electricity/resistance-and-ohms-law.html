Current Electricity, lesson 2

Resistance and Ohm's Law

The same cell gives a big current through a copper wire and almost none through a rubber band. The difference is resistance, and Ohm's law tells us exactly how it controls the current.

What resistance is

As free electrons drift through a wire, they keep colliding with the atoms of the metal. These collisions oppose their flow. This opposition is called resistance (R), measured in ohms (Ω).

Ohm's law

If the physical state of a conductor (such as its temperature) stays the same, the current through it is directly proportional to the potential difference across it.
$$V = IR \qquad\text{or}\qquad R = \frac{V}{I}$$

A conductor has a resistance of 1 ohm if a potential difference of 1 volt across it drives a current of 1 ampere: \(1\ \Omega = 1\ \text{V/A}\).

The experiment

The simulation reproduces the textbook experiment. A nichrome wire XY is joined to cells through a plug key and an ammeter in series; a voltmeter is joined across XY. Record a reading with 1, 2, 3 and 4 cells. The points lie on a straight line through the origin, so \(I \propto V\), and \(V \div I\) is the same every time: that constant is the resistance.

What decides resistance

For a wire of length \(L\) and cross-sectional area \(A\):

$$R = \rho\,\frac{L}{A}$$
  • Length: double the length, double the resistance.
  • Thickness: a thicker wire (larger \(A\)) has less resistance. Doubling the diameter makes \(A\) four times larger, so \(R\) drops to a quarter.
  • Material: each material has its own resistivity \(\rho\), measured in ohm metre (Ω m).
  • Temperature: for most metals, resistance rises as they get hotter.

Conductors and insulators

MaterialResistivity (Ω m)Type and use
Copper1.7 × 10⁻⁸Conductor: connecting wires
Aluminium2.7 × 10⁻⁸Conductor: overhead power lines
Nichrome1.1 × 10⁻⁶Conductor with high resistance: heater and iron elements
Glassabout 10¹⁰ to 10¹⁴Insulator
Rubberabout 10¹³Insulator: gloves, cable covering

Conductors have very low resistance, so current flows through them easily. Insulators have extremely high resistance, so practically no current flows. Wires are made of copper and covered in plastic or rubber, so current flows along the wire but cannot leak out.

The 2 m wire has twice the resistance (31.1 Ω), so its line is half as steep. The 0.6 mm wire has a quarter of the resistance (3.9 Ω), so its line is four times as steep. Rubber lets practically no current through.

Worked examples

1. An electric iron

An electric iron draws a current of 4 A from the 230 V mains. Find the resistance of its element.

  1. \(R = V / I = 230 / 4 = \) 57.5 Ω

2. A resistor and a cell

A 5 Ω resistor is joined across a 1.5 V cell. What current flows?

  1. \(I = V / R = 1.5 / 5 = \) 0.3 A

3. Resistance of a nichrome wire

Find the resistance of a nichrome wire 1 m long and 0.3 mm in diameter. (ρ = 1.1 × 10⁻⁶ Ω m)

  1. Radius \(r = 0.15\) mm \(= 1.5 \times 10^{-4}\) m, so \(A = \pi r^2 = 3.14 \times (1.5 \times 10^{-4})^2 \approx 7.07 \times 10^{-8}\) m²
  2. \(R = \rho L / A = \dfrac{1.1 \times 10^{-6} \times 1}{7.07 \times 10^{-8}} \approx \) 15.6 Ω

Common mistakes

  • Using the diameter as the radius. A = πr². Halve the diameter first, and convert mm to m.
  • Thinking Ohm's law always holds. It needs a constant physical state. A bulb filament heats up, so its resistance changes.
  • Doubling thickness halves R. Doubling the diameter makes the area four times larger, so R falls to a quarter.
  • Mixing up resistance and resistivity. Resistance (Ω) belongs to one wire. Resistivity (Ω m) belongs to the material.

Quick check

1. A potential difference of 12 V drives a current of 0.5 A through a resistor. Its resistance is…

\(R = V / I = 12 / 0.5 = 24\ \Omega\).

2. On a graph of current against potential difference for a wire obeying Ohm's law, the points lie on…

Current is directly proportional to potential difference.

3. If a wire is replaced by one of the same material, twice as long and equally thick, its resistance…

\(R \propto L\).

4. Which material is used for the heating element of an electric iron?

Nichrome has a high resistivity and does not burn easily when hot.
