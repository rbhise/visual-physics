Circle, lesson 2

Incircle and Circumcircle

Every triangle has exactly one circle through its three vertices and exactly one circle that touches its three sides. The bisectors from Chapter 4 find the centres of both.

The circumcircle

The circle that passes through all three vertices of a triangle is its circumcircle. Its centre is the circumcentre and its radius is the circumradius.

The perpendicular bisectors of the sides of a triangle are concurrent. They meet in one point, the circumcentre. Any point on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\) is equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\); the point \(O\) on the bisectors of \(AB\) and \(BC\) has \(OA = OB = OC\). So \(O\) is also on the bisector of \(CA\), and the circle with centre \(O\) and radius \(OA\) passes through \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\).

To construct it: draw the triangle; draw the perpendicular bisectors of any two sides; mark their meeting point \(O\); draw the circle with centre \(O\) and radius \(OA\).

Where is the circumcentre?

TriangleCircumcentre
Acute-angledinside the triangle
Right-angledat the midpoint of the hypotenuse
Obtuse-angledoutside the triangle

In a right-angled triangle the circumradius is half the hypotenuse. This is the same fact as the median on the hypotenuse from Chapter 3.

The incircle

The circle that touches all three sides of a triangle from the inside is its incircle. Its centre is the incentre and its radius is the inradius.

The angle bisectors of a triangle are concurrent. They meet in one point, the incentre \(I\). A point on the bisector of \(\angle B\) is equidistant from \(BA\) and \(BC\); a point on the bisector of \(\angle C\) is equidistant from \(CB\) and \(CA\). So \(I\) is the same distance from all three sides.

To construct it: draw the triangle; bisect any two angles; mark their meeting point \(I\); draw \(IP \perp BC\); draw the circle with centre \(I\) and radius \(IP\). The incentre is always inside the triangle.

What you should see: the circumcentre is inside the acute triangle, at the midpoint of the 10 cm hypotenuse (radius 5 cm) of the right triangle, and outside the obtuse triangle. The incircle of the 6, 8, 10 triangle has radius 2 cm, and its centre is inside, as always.

Worked examples

1. Circumradius of a right triangle

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(\angle B = 90^\circ\), \(AB = 6\) cm and \(BC = 8\) cm. Where is the circumcentre, and what is the circumradius?

  1. \(AC = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10\) cm, the hypotenuse.
  2. In a right triangle the circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse.
  3. O is the midpoint of AC; circumradius = 5 cm

2. An equilateral triangle

Draw the incircle and circumcircle of an equilateral triangle of side 6 cm. Compare the radii.

  1. In an equilateral triangle each angle bisector is also the perpendicular bisector of the opposite side, so the incentre and circumcentre are the same point.
  2. Measuring (or using the sim): circumradius ≈ 3.46 cm, inradius ≈ 1.73 cm.
  3. circumradius : inradius = 2 : 1

3. Inside or outside?

Where is the circumcentre of a triangle with angles (a) \(50^\circ, 60^\circ, 70^\circ\); (b) \(30^\circ, 60^\circ, 90^\circ\); (c) \(20^\circ, 35^\circ, 125^\circ\)?

  1. (a) All angles are less than 90°: acute, so inside.
  2. (b) One angle is 90°: on the hypotenuse, at its midpoint.
  3. (c) 125° is obtuse, so outside.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up the bisectors. Circumcircle: perpendicular bisectors of the sides. Incircle: bisectors of the angles.
  • Wrong radius for the incircle. The inradius is the perpendicular IP from I to a side, not IA.
  • Expecting the circumcentre inside. For an obtuse triangle it is outside; for a right triangle it is on the hypotenuse.
  • Drawing all three bisectors to find the centre. Two are enough; the third passes through the same point and is a check.

Quick check

1. The circumcentre of a triangle is the point where these meet:

Each perpendicular bisector holds the points equidistant from two vertices.

2. A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 13 cm. Its circumradius is…

The circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, so the radius is 13 ÷ 2.

3. The incentre of a triangle is equidistant from…

It lies on all three angle bisectors, so it is the same distance from all three sides.

4. The circumcentre of a triangle with angles 25°, 40° and 115° lies…

The triangle is obtuse-angled (115°), so the circumcentre is outside.
