Angles Made by a Transversal

Railway tracks, the lines of a ruled notebook, the edges of a ladder: parallel lines are everywhere. Cut them with one more line and eight angles appear, tied together by simple rules.

Parallel lines and a transversal

Lines in one plane that do not intersect each other are parallel lines. We write \(l \parallel m\).

Transversal. If a line intersects two lines in two distinct points, it is called a transversal of those lines.

In the simulation, transversal \(t\) cuts line \(l\) and line \(m\). It makes four angles at each point: \(\angle a, \angle b, \angle c, \angle d\) at \(l\) and \(\angle p, \angle q, \angle r, \angle s\) at \(m\). The angles between the two lines (\(\angle c, \angle d, \angle p, \angle q\)) are interior angles; the other four are exterior angles.

Properties of parallel lines

Interior angle theorem. If two parallel lines are intersected by a transversal, the interior angles on either side of the transversal are supplementary.

Why: by Euclid’s postulate, if the interior angles on one side add up to less than 180°, the lines meet on that side. Turn line \(m\) in the simulation and watch this happen. Parallel lines never meet, so neither sum is less than 180°. The two sums together make \(\angle c + \angle d + \angle p + \angle q = 360^\circ\), so each sum is exactly \(180^\circ\).

Corresponding angle theorem. The corresponding angles formed by a transversal of two parallel lines are of equal measure.
Alternate angle theorem. The alternate angles formed by a transversal of two parallel lines are of equal measure.

Proof (direct): \(\angle a + \angle d = 180^\circ\) (linear pair) and \(\angle d + \angle p = 180^\circ\) (interior angle theorem), so \(\angle a = \angle p\). Then \(\angle c = \angle a\) (vertically opposite angles), so \(\angle c = \angle p\).

One more consequence: if a line is perpendicular to one of two parallel lines, it is perpendicular to the other too. (If \(\angle a = 90^\circ\), then its corresponding angle \(\angle p = 90^\circ\).)

The angle sum of a triangle

Draw line PQ through A parallel to side BC of \(\triangle ABC\). With transversal AB, \(\angle PAB = \angle ABC\) (alternate angles). With transversal AC, \(\angle QAC = \angle ACB\). The angles \(\angle PAB\), \(\angle BAC\) and \(\angle QAC\) together make the straight angle PAQ, so

$$\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ$$

Try it

Choose a kind of pair. Drag the top of t, or turn line m with the slider or by its end.

  • Corresponding. l ∥ m, transversal at 60°.
  • Alternate interior. l ∥ m, transversal at 70°.
  • Interior angles. l ∥ m, transversal at 65°.
  • Not parallel. Turn m by 10°.
  • Angle sum. ∠B = 55°, ∠C = 70°.

What you should see: with l ∥ m, ∠a = ∠p = 60° and ∠d = ∠s = 120°; alternate angles ∠c = ∠p = 70°; interior angles 115° + 65° = 180°. When m is turned by 10°, ∠p becomes 50° and is no longer equal to ∠a; the interior angles on the right add up to 170° and the lines meet on the right. In the triangle, ∠A = 55° and 55° + 55° + 70° = 180°.

Worked examples

1. All eight angles from one

\(l \parallel m\) and \(\angle a = 75^\circ\). Find the other seven angles.

  1. \(\angle b = 180^\circ - 75^\circ = 105^\circ\) (linear pair); \(\angle c = 75^\circ\), \(\angle d = 105^\circ\) (vertically opposite angles).
  2. Corresponding angles are equal: \(\angle p = \angle a\), \(\angle q = \angle b\), \(\angle r = \angle c\), \(\angle s = \angle d\).
  3. ∠a = ∠c = ∠p = ∠r = 75°; ∠b = ∠d = ∠q = ∠s = 105°

2. Interior angles with x

\(l \parallel m\), \(\angle d = (3x + 10)^\circ\) and \(\angle p = (2x + 20)^\circ\). Find \(x\), \(\angle d\) and \(\angle p\).

  1. ∠d and ∠p are interior angles on the same side, so they are supplementary: \(3x + 10 + 2x + 20 = 180\).
  2. \(5x + 30 = 180\), so \(5x = 150\) and x = 30
  3. \(\angle d = 3(30) + 10 = \) 100° and \(\angle p = 2(30) + 20 = \) 80°

3. A line parallel to the base

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(\angle B = 48^\circ\) and \(\angle C = 67^\circ\). Line PQ through A is parallel to BC. Find \(\angle PAB\), \(\angle QAC\) and \(\angle BAC\).

  1. \(\angle PAB = \angle ABC = 48^\circ\) and \(\angle QAC = \angle ACB = 67^\circ\) (alternate angles).
  2. \(\angle PAB + \angle BAC + \angle QAC = 180^\circ\), so \(\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 48^\circ - 67^\circ = \) 65°

Common mistakes

  • Using the properties for any two lines. Corresponding angles are equal only when the lines are parallel. Check that l ∥ m is given.
  • “Interior angles are equal.” Interior angles on the same side are supplementary: they add up to 180°.
  • Mixing up the pairs. Corresponding angles sit in the same position at the two points. Alternate angles are on opposite sides of the transversal.
  • Reading the wrong angle. Name each angle by its letter or with three points, like ∠PAB, before you use a property.

Quick check

1. l ∥ m. One of a pair of corresponding angles is 112°. The other one is…

Corresponding angles formed by parallel lines are equal.

2. l ∥ m. One interior angle is 75°. The interior angle on the same side of the transversal is…

Interior angles on the same side are supplementary: 180° − 75° = 105°.

3. In this lesson’s figure, which pair is a pair of alternate interior angles?

∠c and ∠p lie between the lines, on opposite sides of t. ∠a, ∠p are corresponding; ∠d, ∠p are interior on the same side; ∠a, ∠r are alternate exterior.

4. In △ABC, ∠B = 40° and ∠C = 75°. Line PQ through A is parallel to BC, with Q on the side of C. Then ∠QAC is…

∠QAC and ∠ACB are alternate angles, so ∠QAC = 75°.
