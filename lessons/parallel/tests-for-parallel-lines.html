Parallel Lines, lesson 2

Tests for Parallel Lines

The properties tell us about angles when we already know the lines are parallel. The tests work the other way round: measure a pair of angles and decide whether the lines are parallel.

Properties and tests

In the last lesson we started from \(l \parallel m\) and found facts about the angles. Those were conditional statements. Their converses start from the angles and conclude that the lines are parallel. The converses are also true, and they are called the tests for parallel lines.

The three tests

Interior angle test. If the interior angles formed by a transversal of two distinct lines are supplementary, then the two lines are parallel.
Alternate angle test. If a pair of alternate angles formed by a transversal of two lines is congruent, then the two lines are parallel.
Corresponding angle test. If a pair of corresponding angles formed by a transversal of two lines is congruent, then the two lines are parallel.

The simulation uses the same letters as before: \(\angle a, \angle b, \angle c, \angle d\) at \(l\) and \(\angle p, \angle q, \angle r, \angle s\) at \(m\). Set \(\angle p\) and watch: the lines become parallel exactly when the pair passes the test.

Why the interior angle test works (indirect proof)

Suppose \(\angle d + \angle p = 180^\circ\) but the lines are not parallel. Then they meet at some point T, on one side of the transversal. The other two interior angles also add up to \(360^\circ - 180^\circ = 180^\circ\). So whichever side T is on, the triangle formed there has two angles adding up to \(180^\circ\), and its third angle at T would be \(0^\circ\). That is impossible. So the lines are parallel.

The other two tests follow from it. If \(\angle c = \angle p\), then \(\angle d + \angle p = \angle d + \angle c = 180^\circ\) (linear pair), so the interior angle test applies. If \(\angle a = \angle p\), then \(\angle c = \angle a = \angle p\) (vertically opposite angles), so the alternate angle test applies.

Two corollaries

  • If a line is perpendicular to two lines in a plane, then the two lines are parallel to each other. (The interior angles are \(90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ\).)
  • If two lines in a plane are parallel to a third line in the plane, then those two lines are parallel to each other.

Try it

Choose a test, then set ∠p with the slider or by dragging the end of line m.

  • Corresponding, not equal. ∠a = 65°, ∠p = 50°.
  • Corresponding, equal. ∠a = 65°, ∠p = 65°.
  • Alternate. ∠c = 110°, ∠p = 110°.
  • Interior. ∠d = 110°, ∠p = 70°.
  • Both perpendicular. l ⊥ t and m ⊥ t.

What you should see: with 65° and 50° the test fails and the lines meet on the right. With 65° and 65°, 110° and 110°, or 110° + 70° = 180°, the test passes and the lines are parallel. Two lines perpendicular to t are parallel.

Worked examples

1. Name the test

A transversal makes \(\angle a = 72^\circ\) with line \(l\) and the corresponding angle \(\angle p = 72^\circ\) with line \(m\). Are the lines parallel?

  1. \(\angle a\) and \(\angle p\) are corresponding angles and they are equal.
  2. By the corresponding angle test, l ∥ m

2. Find x so that the lines are parallel

Interior angles on the same side of a transversal are \(\angle d = (2x + 16)^\circ\) and \(\angle p = (3x - 6)^\circ\). For what value of \(x\) is \(l \parallel m\)?

  1. By the interior angle test the lines are parallel when \(2x + 16 + 3x - 6 = 180\).
  2. \(5x + 10 = 180\), so \(5x = 170\) and x = 34
  3. Then \(\angle d = 84^\circ\) and \(\angle p = 96^\circ\); \(84^\circ + 96^\circ = 180^\circ\) ✓

3. Using the corollaries

Lines \(l\) and \(m\) are both perpendicular to line \(n\). Line \(k\) is parallel to \(m\). How are \(k\) and \(l\) related?

  1. \(l \perp n\) and \(m \perp n\), so \(l \parallel m\) (a line perpendicular to two lines).
  2. \(k \parallel m\) and \(l \parallel m\): two lines parallel to a third line are parallel to each other.
  3. So k ∥ l

Common mistakes

  • Testing the wrong pair. Vertically opposite angles are always equal; they say nothing about whether the lines are parallel. Use a pair at the two different lines.
  • “Equal interior angles, so parallel.” Interior angles on the same side must add up to 180°. They are equal only when both are 90°.
  • Mixing up property and test. A property starts from l ∥ m. A test ends with l ∥ m. Say which one you are using.
  • Wrong positions. Alternate angles must be on opposite sides of the transversal; interior angles of the test must be on the same side.

Quick check

1. A transversal makes a pair of alternate angles of 63° and 63°. The two lines are…

Equal alternate angles: by the alternate angle test the lines are parallel.

2. Interior angles on the same side of a transversal are 95° and 95°. The lines are…

95° + 95° = 190°, not 180°. The interior angles on the other side add up to 170°, so the lines meet on that side.

3. Corresponding angles are (3x + 5)° and 50°. The lines are parallel when x is…

3x + 5 = 50, so 3x = 45 and x = 15.

4. Line l ⊥ line n and line m ⊥ line n, all in one plane. Then…

A line perpendicular to two lines in a plane makes them parallel (interior angles 90° + 90° = 180°).
