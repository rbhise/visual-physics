The Midpoint Theorem

Mark the middle of two sides of any triangle and join them. However you draw the triangle, the new segment is parallel to the third side and exactly half as long.

Download slides (.pptx) 12 slides with teacher notes

The theorem

Midpoint theorem. The segment joining the midpoints of any two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and half of it.
In \(\triangle ABC\), if \(P\) and \(Q\) are the midpoints of \(AB\) and \(AC\), then \(PQ \parallel BC\) and \(PQ = \tfrac12 BC\).

Why it is true

Extend \(PQ\) to a point \(R\) so that \(QR = PQ\), and join \(CR\).

  • In \(\triangle AQP\) and \(\triangle CQR\): \(AQ = CQ\), \(PQ = RQ\), and the vertically opposite angles at \(Q\) are equal. So \(\triangle AQP \cong \triangle CQR\) by the S-A-S test.
  • So \(CR = AP = PB\), and \(\angle QAP = \angle QCR\). These are alternate angles, so \(CR \parallel AB\).
  • In \(\square PBCR\), the pair \(PB\) and \(CR\) is parallel and congruent, so it is a parallelogram. Hence \(PR \parallel BC\) and \(PR = BC\).
  • \(PQ\) is half of \(PR\), so \(PQ = \tfrac12 BC\).

Press “Show why” in the simulation: \(\triangle APQ\) turns half a turn about \(Q\) and lands exactly on \(\triangle CRQ\).

The converse

Converse of the midpoint theorem. If a line drawn through the midpoint of one side of a triangle is parallel to another side, it bisects the third side.

Uses

Midpoints of a quadrilateral. Join the midpoints \(P, Q, R, S\) of the sides of any quadrilateral \(ABCD\). In \(\triangle ABC\), \(PQ \parallel AC\) and \(PQ = \tfrac12 AC\). In \(\triangle ADC\), \(SR \parallel AC\) and \(SR = \tfrac12 AC\). So \(PQ\) and \(SR\) are parallel and congruent, and \(PQRS\) is always a parallelogram.

Trapezium. In trapezium \(ABCD\) with \(AB \parallel DC\), the segment \(MN\) joining the midpoints of the non-parallel sides is parallel to the parallel sides and

$$MN = \tfrac12\,(AB + DC)$$

Worked examples

1. Find the segment

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(P\) and \(Q\) are the midpoints of \(AB\) and \(AC\), and \(BC = 9\) cm. Find \(PQ\).

  1. By the midpoint theorem, \(PQ = \tfrac12 BC = \tfrac12 \times 9\).
  2. PQ = 4.5 cm, and \(PQ \parallel BC\).

2. The midpoint triangle

The sides of \(\triangle XYZ\) are 8 cm, 10 cm and 12 cm. Find the perimeter of the triangle formed by joining the midpoints of its sides.

  1. Each side of the new triangle joins two midpoints, so it is half of the third side: 4 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm.
  2. Perimeter \(= 4 + 5 + 6 = 15\) cm, which is half of \(8 + 10 + 12 = 30\) cm.
  3. 15 cm

3. A trapezium

In trapezium \(ABCD\), \(AB \parallel DC\), \(AB = 12\) cm and \(DC = 6\) cm. \(M\) and \(N\) are the midpoints of \(AD\) and \(BC\). Find \(MN\).

  1. \(MN = \tfrac12 (AB + DC) = \tfrac12 (12 + 6)\).
  2. MN = 9 cm

Common mistakes

  • PQ equals BC. PQ is half of BC. Going the other way, BC is double PQ.
  • Parallel to the wrong side. PQ is parallel to the third side: the one that does not contain P or Q.
  • Any parallel segment is half. Only the segment through the midpoints. A parallel line nearer to A gives a shorter segment.
  • Mixing up the converse. In the converse you start with one midpoint and a parallel line, and prove the other point is a midpoint.

Quick check

1. P and Q are the midpoints of sides AB and AC of △ABC, and PQ = 7 cm. Then BC is…

PQ = ½ BC, so BC = 2 × 7 = 14 cm.

2. In △ABC, a line through the midpoint of AB, parallel to BC, meets AC at Q. If AC = 10 cm, AQ is…

By the converse, Q is the midpoint of AC: AQ = 5 cm.

3. Joining the midpoints of the sides of any quadrilateral, in order, always gives…

Two opposite sides are both parallel to one diagonal and half of it, so they are parallel and congruent.

4. A triangle has sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm. The triangle formed by joining their midpoints has perimeter…

Its sides are 3, 4 and 5 cm: half of 24 cm.
