Quadrilaterals, lesson 2

Rectangle, Rhombus, Square and Trapezium

A door is a rectangle, a kite-shaped tile is often a rhombus, and a bucket seen from the side is an isosceles trapezium. Each special quadrilateral adds one extra condition, and each condition changes what the diagonals do.

Definitions

Rectangle. If all angles of a quadrilateral are right angles, it is called a rectangle.

Rhombus. If all sides of a quadrilateral are congruent, it is called a rhombus.

Square. If all sides and all angles of a quadrilateral are congruent, it is called a square.

Trapezium. If only one pair of opposite sides of a quadrilateral is parallel, it is called a trapezium.

Isosceles trapezium. If the non-parallel sides of a trapezium are congruent, it is called an isosceles trapezium.

A rectangle, a rhombus and a square all have both pairs of opposite sides parallel, so each one is a parallelogram and has every property from the last lesson. A square is a rectangle and a rhombus at the same time. A trapezium is not a parallelogram.

Theorems. The diagonals of a rectangle are congruent. The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular bisectors of each other, and they bisect its opposite angles. The diagonals of a square are congruent, are perpendicular bisectors of each other, and bisect its opposite angles.

Trapeziums

In trapezium \(ABCD\) with \(AB \parallel DC\), side \(AD\) is a transversal of the two parallel sides, so \(\angle A + \angle D = 180^\circ\). In the same way \(\angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ\).

Isosceles trapezium. The base angles of an isosceles trapezium are congruent: if \(AD = BC\), then \(\angle A = \angle B\) and \(\angle D = \angle C\). Its diagonals are congruent too.

The simulation also knows a kite: two pairs of equal sides next to each other. Its diagonals are perpendicular, but only one of them is bisected.

Worked examples

1. Diagonals of a rectangle

In rectangle \(ABCD\), \(AB = 8\) cm and \(BC = 6\) cm. The diagonals meet at \(O\). Find \(AC\), \(BD\) and \(AO\).

  1. \(\angle B = 90^\circ\), so by Pythagoras \(AC = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10\) cm.
  2. The diagonals of a rectangle are congruent: \(BD = 10\) cm. They bisect each other: \(AO = 5\) cm.
  3. AC = BD = 10 cm, AO = 5 cm

2. Side of a rhombus

The diagonals of a rhombus are 8 cm and 6 cm long. Find its side and its perimeter.

  1. The diagonals are perpendicular bisectors of each other, so they make four right triangles with legs 4 cm and 3 cm.
  2. Side \(= \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5\) cm. Perimeter \(= 4 \times 5 = 20\) cm.
  3. side 5 cm, perimeter 20 cm

3. Angles of an isosceles trapezium

In trapezium \(PQRS\), \(PQ \parallel SR\) and \(PS = QR\). If \(\angle P = 65^\circ\), find the other angles.

  1. Base angles of an isosceles trapezium are congruent: \(\angle Q = \angle P = 65^\circ\).
  2. \(\angle P + \angle S = 180^\circ\) (interior angles, \(PQ \parallel SR\)), so \(\angle S = 115^\circ\), and \(\angle R = \angle S\).
  3. ∠Q = 65°, ∠R = ∠S = 115°

Common mistakes

  • A rhombus has equal diagonals. No. Its diagonals are perpendicular. Equal diagonals belong to a rectangle (and a square).
  • A square is not a rectangle. It is: all its angles are right angles. It is also a rhombus.
  • Every trapezium has equal base angles. Only an isosceles trapezium does.
  • Perpendicular diagonals make a rhombus. A kite has them too. For a rhombus the diagonals must also bisect each other.

Quick check

1. The diagonals of a rhombus are 10 cm and 24 cm. Its side is…

Half-diagonals 5 and 12 meet at 90°: √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13 cm.

2. Which statement is true?

A square has all sides congruent, so it is a rhombus. The others fail: a rhombus need not have right angles, and so on.

3. In square ABCD, ∠ABD is…

The diagonal BD bisects the 90° angle at B.

4. In rectangle PQRS the diagonals meet at O, and PR = 18 cm. Then QO is…

QS = PR = 18 cm (congruent diagonals), and O is the midpoint of QS.
