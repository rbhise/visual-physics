Quadrilaterals, lesson 1

Parallelograms

Push a rectangular gate sideways and it leans into a slanted shape, but its opposite bars stay parallel. That shape is a parallelogram, and it has a set of properties you can rely on.

What is a parallelogram?

A quadrilateral \(\square ABCD\) has four sides, four angles and two diagonals, \(AC\) and \(BD\). Sides \(AB\) and \(DC\) are opposite sides; sides \(AB\) and \(BC\) are adjacent sides. In the same way \(\angle A\) and \(\angle C\) are opposite angles, and \(\angle A\) and \(\angle B\) are adjacent angles.

Parallelogram. A quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite sides are parallel is called a parallelogram. In \(\square ABCD\): \(AB \parallel DC\) and \(AD \parallel BC\).

Properties of a parallelogram

Theorems.
  1. Opposite sides of a parallelogram are congruent.
  2. Opposite angles of a parallelogram are congruent.
  3. Diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

Why? Draw diagonal \(AC\). Because \(AB \parallel DC\), the alternate angles \(\angle BAC\) and \(\angle DCA\) are equal. Because \(AD \parallel BC\), \(\angle DAC = \angle BCA\). \(AC\) is common, so \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA\) by the A-S-A test. Matching parts give \(AB = CD\), \(BC = DA\) and \(\angle B = \angle D\).

Also, \(\angle A\) and \(\angle B\) are interior angles on the same side of the transversal \(AB\) between the parallel lines \(AD\) and \(BC\). So adjacent angles of a parallelogram are supplementary: they add up to \(180^\circ\).

Tests for a parallelogram

The theorems also work the other way round. A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if any one of these is true:

  • both pairs of opposite sides are congruent;
  • both pairs of opposite angles are congruent;
  • the diagonals bisect each other;
  • one pair of opposite sides is parallel and congruent.

In the simulation, the arrows > mark parallel sides, short ticks mark equal sides, matching arcs mark equal angles and small circles mark equal halves of the diagonals.

Try it

Drag the corners A, B, C, D (they snap to the grid), or use the buttons. The readouts compare opposite sides, opposite angles and the halves of the diagonals.

  • A parallelogram. Are the opposite sides and angles equal?
  • Keep it a parallelogram. Drag A, B or C: D follows. Do the properties ever fail?
  • Any quadrilateral. No parallel sides at all.
  • A trapezium. One pair parallel is not enough.

What you should see: in the parallelogram, AB = CD = 7 cm, BC = DA ≈ 5.83 cm, ∠A = ∠C ≈ 59.0° and ∠B = ∠D ≈ 121.0°, so ∠A + ∠B = 180°. The diagonals cut each other into equal halves at O. With the lock on, every shape you make keeps all these properties. The other two shapes fail every test.

Worked examples

1. Angles of a parallelogram

In parallelogram \(PQRS\), \(\angle P = 70^\circ\). Find the other three angles.

  1. Opposite angles are congruent: \(\angle R = \angle P = 70^\circ\).
  2. Adjacent angles are supplementary: \(\angle Q = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ\), and \(\angle S = \angle Q\).
  3. ∠R = 70°, ∠Q = ∠S = 110°

2. Sides from the perimeter

The perimeter of parallelogram \(ABCD\) is 64 cm and \(AB : BC = 5 : 3\). Find all four sides.

  1. Opposite sides are equal, so perimeter \(= 2(AB + BC) = 64\) and \(AB + BC = 32\).
  2. Let \(AB = 5k\), \(BC = 3k\): \(8k = 32\), so \(k = 4\).
  3. AB = CD = 20 cm, BC = AD = 12 cm

3. Use a test

On a centimetre grid, \(A\) is at the corner (1, 1), \(B\) at (7, 2), \(C\) at (10, 6) and \(D\) at (4, 5). Is \(\square ABCD\) a parallelogram?

  1. From \(A\) to \(B\): 6 squares right and 1 up. From \(D\) to \(C\): also 6 right and 1 up.
  2. So \(AB\) and \(DC\) point the same way and have the same length: they are parallel and congruent.
  3. One pair of opposite sides is parallel and congruent, so ABCD is a parallelogram

The simulation confirms it: \(BC = DA = 5\) cm, \(\angle A = \angle C \approx 43.7^\circ\), and the diagonals bisect each other.

Common mistakes

  • Adjacent angles are equal. In a parallelogram the opposite angles are equal. Adjacent angles add up to 180°.
  • The diagonals are equal. Not in every parallelogram. They always bisect each other, but they are equal only in a rectangle or a square.
  • One pair of parallel sides is enough. That only makes a trapezium. The same pair must be parallel and congruent, or both pairs must be parallel.
  • Mixing up opposite and adjacent. In □ABCD, side AB is opposite DC, not BC. Read the letters in order round the shape.

Quick check

1. In parallelogram ABCD, ∠B = 125°. Then ∠D is…

∠B and ∠D are opposite angles, so they are congruent.

2. In parallelogram ABCD, ∠A = 72°. Then ∠B is…

Adjacent angles are supplementary: 180° − 72° = 108°.

3. The diagonals of parallelogram PQRS meet at O, and PO = 6 cm. Then PR is…

The diagonals bisect each other, so OR = PO = 6 cm and PR = 12 cm.

4. Which of these is not enough to prove a quadrilateral is a parallelogram?

A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides and is not a parallelogram.
