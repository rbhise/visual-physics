Financial Planning, lesson 2

Income Tax

Roads, schools, hospitals and the army are paid for from taxes. Income tax is the tax a person pays on what they earn in a year, and it is worked out slab by slab.

Download slides (.pptx) 12 slides with teacher notes

Why taxes?

The government needs money for public services: roads, railways, schools, hospitals, defence, police and help for the poor. It collects this money mainly as taxes. Paying taxes honestly is every citizen's duty.

  • Direct tax: paid directly to the government by the person who owes it. Income tax is a direct tax.
  • Indirect tax: collected by a seller from the buyer as part of the price, and passed on to the government. GST on goods and services is an indirect tax.

Income tax and PAN

Income tax is charged on the income a person earns in a financial year, which runs from 1 April to 31 March. The income is assessed in the next year, the assessment year: for the financial year 2017–18 the assessment year is 2018–19.

Every taxpayer has a PAN (Permanent Account Number): a 10-character code of letters and digits issued by the Income Tax Department. It is needed to file the yearly income tax return and for large money transactions.

Taxable income

Some savings and donations can be subtracted from the total income before tax is worked out. These are deductions.

  • Section 80C: savings such as PPF, GPF, LIC premium, NSC and children's tuition fees. At most ₹1,50,000 can be deducted in total.
  • Section 80G: donations to approved funds, such as the Prime Minister's National Relief Fund.
Taxable income = Total income − Deductions.

The slab table

Income tax is worked out in slabs: each band of income has its own rate, and each rate applies only to the part of the income inside that band. These are the slab rates used in your textbook (financial year 2017–18):

Where does ₹12,500 come from? It is the tax on the 5% slab when it is full: 5% of ₹2,50,000. And ₹1,12,500 = ₹12,500 + 20% of ₹5,00,000.

On top of the income tax, the textbook adds education cess at 2% of the income tax and secondary and higher education cess at 1% of the income tax. (Very high incomes also pay a surcharge; we do not need it here.)

Rates change. The slabs, the cess and the rebates are changed in the Union Budget, and they have changed many times since 2017–18. Learn the method with the textbook's table; the current year's rates are on the Income Tax Department website, incometax.gov.in.

Worked examples

1. The 5% slab

Ms Joshi is 32. Her taxable income is ₹4,20,000. Find the tax she must pay.

  1. ₹4,20,000 is in the slab ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 (age below 60).
  2. Income tax \(= 5\%\) of \((4,20,000 - 2,50,000) = \tfrac{5}{100} \times 1,70,000 = 8,500\).
  3. Education cess \(= 2\%\) of 8,500 \(= 170\); secondary and higher education cess \(= 1\%\) of 8,500 \(= 85\).
  4. Total tax \(= 8,500 + 170 + 85 = \) ₹8,755

2. Deductions first

Mr Patil is 45. His yearly income is ₹9,60,000. He pays ₹1,00,000 into PPF and ₹70,000 as LIC premium, and gives ₹10,000 to the Prime Minister's National Relief Fund. Find his income tax with cess.

  1. 80C savings are ₹1,70,000, but at most ₹1,50,000 is allowed. Deductions \(= 1,50,000 + 10,000 = 1,60,000\).
  2. Taxable income \(= 9,60,000 - 1,60,000 = 8,00,000\): slab ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000.
  3. Income tax \(= 12,500 + 20\%\) of \((8,00,000 - 5,00,000) = 12,500 + 60,000 = 72,500\).
  4. Cess \(= 2\%\) of 72,500 + \(1\%\) of 72,500 \(= 1,450 + 725 = 2,175\).
  5. Total tax \(= 72,500 + 2,175 = \) ₹74,675

3. A senior citizen

Mrs Rao is 68. Her taxable income is ₹12,40,000. Find her total tax.

  1. Age 60 to 80, income above ₹10,00,000.
  2. Income tax \(= 1,10,000 + 30\%\) of \((12,40,000 - 10,00,000) = 1,10,000 + 72,000 = 1,82,000\).
  3. Cess: \(2\%\) of 1,82,000 \(= 3,640\) and \(1\%\) of 1,82,000 \(= 1,820\).
  4. Total tax \(= 1,82,000 + 3,640 + 1,820 = \) ₹1,87,460

Common mistakes

  • Taxing the whole income at the slab rate. 5% applies only to the part above ₹2,50,000 (or ₹3,00,000), not to the whole income.
  • Deducting all the 80C savings. Only ₹1,50,000 can be deducted under 80C, however much is saved.
  • Working out cess on the income. Cess is 2% and 1% of the income tax, not of the income.
  • Using the wrong age column. Check the taxpayer's age first: the nil limit is ₹2,50,000, ₹3,00,000 or ₹5,00,000.

Quick check

1. Which of these is a direct tax?

Income tax is paid directly to the government by the person who earns the income. The others are collected by the seller from the buyer.

2. Using the textbook slabs, Mr Shah (age 40) has a taxable income of ₹3,60,000. What is his income tax before cess?

5% of (3,60,000 − 2,50,000) = 5% of 1,10,000 = ₹5,500.

3. Using the textbook slabs, Mrs Kulkarni (age 70) has a taxable income of ₹2,90,000. Her income tax is…

For age 60 to 80, income up to ₹3,00,000 is in the nil slab.

4. A person's income tax is ₹20,000. With education cess 2% and secondary and higher education cess 1%, the total cess is…

2% of 20,000 = 400 and 1% of 20,000 = 200; together ₹600.
