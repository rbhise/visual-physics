Ratio and Proportion, lesson 1

Ratio, Direct and Inverse Proportion

A recipe needs 2 cups of rice for every 3 cups of water. Double the rice and you double the water. Ratio tells you how big one quantity is compared with another.

Ratio

Ratio. For two numbers \(a\) and \(b\) with \(b \ne 0\), the ratio of \(a\) to \(b\) is written \(a : b\) or \(\dfrac{a}{b}\). \(a\) is the first term (antecedent) and \(b\) is the second term (consequent).

A ratio compares quantities of the same kind, in the same unit. The ratio of 1.5 m to 75 cm is \(150 : 75 = 2 : 1\), not \(1.5 : 75\). A ratio has no unit of its own.

Multiplying or dividing both terms by the same non-zero number does not change a ratio: \(a : b = ak : bk\). To write a ratio in simplest form, divide both terms by their HCF:

$$24 : 36 = \frac{24}{12} : \frac{36}{12} = 2 : 3$$

The order matters: \(2 : 3\) and \(3 : 2\) are different ratios.

Comparing ratios

For positive numbers \(a, b, c, d\), compare \(ad\) with \(bc\):

  • if \(ad > bc\), then \(\dfrac{a}{b} > \dfrac{c}{d}\);
  • if \(ad < bc\), then \(\dfrac{a}{b} < \dfrac{c}{d}\);
  • if \(ad = bc\), then \(\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}\), and we say \(a, b, c, d\) are in proportion.

Percentage as a ratio

A percentage is a ratio whose second term is 100: \(x\% = \dfrac{x}{100}\). To change \(3 : 5\) to a percentage, make the second term 100: \(\dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{60}{100} = 60\%\).

Direct and inverse proportion

Direct proportion. If two quantities change so that their ratio stays constant, \(\dfrac{y}{x} = k\), they are in direct proportion: \(y = kx\), written \(y \propto x\).
Inverse proportion. If they change so that their product stays constant, \(xy = k\), they are in inverse proportion: \(y \propto \dfrac{1}{x}\).

The cost of notebooks is in direct proportion to the number bought: 2 notebooks cost twice as much as 1. The number of days needed to finish a job is in inverse proportion to the number of workers: twice the workers need half the days.

Worked examples

1. Same units first

Write the ratio of 45 minutes to 2 hours in simplest form, and as a percentage.

  1. Change to the same unit: 2 hours = 120 minutes, so the ratio is \(45 : 120\).
  2. HCF of 45 and 120 is 15: \(45 : 120 = 3 : 8\). 3 : 8
  3. \(\dfrac{45}{120} \times 100 = 37.5\), so 45 minutes is 37.5% of 2 hours.

2. Comparing ratios

Which ratio is greater, \(\dfrac{5}{7}\) or \(\dfrac{7}{10}\)?

  1. Cross-multiply: \(5 \times 10 = 50\) and \(7 \times 7 = 49\).
  2. \(50 > 49\), so 5/7 > 7/10

3. Inverse proportion

12 workers build a wall in 10 days. Working at the same rate, how many days would 8 workers take?

  1. Fewer workers need more days: inverse proportion. The product stays the same: \(12 \times 10 = 120\).
  2. \(8 \times y = 120\), so \(y = 120 \div 8 = \) 15 days

Common mistakes

  • Mixing units. Change both quantities to the same unit before writing the ratio: 2 hours = 120 minutes.
  • Swapping the order. The ratio of a to b is a : b. Girls to boys 4 : 5 is not boys to girls.
  • Comparing only the tops. 5/7 and 7/10: compare 5 × 10 with 7 × 7, not 5 with 7.
  • Direct or inverse? Ask: if one doubles, does the other double (direct) or halve (inverse)?

Quick check

1. The simplest form of 42 : 56 is…

The HCF of 42 and 56 is 14: 42 ÷ 14 = 3 and 56 ÷ 14 = 4.

2. The ratio of 25 paise to ₹1 is…

₹1 = 100 paise, so 25 : 100 = 1 : 4.

3. Which is greater, 4/9 or 3/7?

4 × 7 = 28 and 9 × 3 = 27. 28 > 27, so 4/9 > 3/7.

4. 6 taps fill a tank in 8 hours. How long would 4 such taps take?

Inverse proportion: 6 × 8 = 48 tap-hours, and 48 ÷ 4 = 12 hours.
