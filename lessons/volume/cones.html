Surface Area and Volume, lesson 2

Cones

An ice-cream cone, a birthday cap, a heap of grain: each is a cone. Its slant height, surface and volume all come from the radius and the height.

Parts of a cone

A right circular cone has a circular base and a pointed top called the vertex. The segment from the vertex to the centre of the base is perpendicular to the base; its length is the perpendicular height \(h\). A segment from the vertex to any point on the edge of the base is a slant height \(l\). The radius of the base is \(r\).

Slant height

The height, the radius and a slant height make a right-angled triangle, with \(l\) as the hypotenuse. By Pythagoras' theorem:

Slant height. $$l^2 = r^2 + h^2$$

Curved surface area

Cut the cone along a slant height and open it out. The curved surface becomes a sector of a circle of radius \(l\); its arc is the circumference of the base, \(2\pi r\). Now cut the sector into many thin pieces and lay them head to tail. They make a shape very close to a rectangle: its length is half the arc, \(\pi r\), and its breadth is \(l\).

Cone. $$\text{Curved surface area} = \pi r \times l = \pi rl$$ $$\text{Total surface area} = \pi rl + \pi r^2 = \pi r(l + r)$$

Volume

Take a hollow cone and a hollow cylinder with the same base and the same height. Fill the cone with sand or water and pour it into the cylinder. It takes exactly three conefuls to fill the cylinder.

Volume of a cone. $$V = \tfrac{1}{3} \times \text{volume of the cylinder} = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$$

Use \(h\), not \(l\), in the volume. Use \(l\), not \(h\), in the curved surface area.

Try it

Use the sliders for r and h, then try each view.

  • Slant height. r = 6 cm, h = 8 cm.
  • Open it out. Unroll the same cone into a sector.
  • Cut and rearrange. Lay 16 thin pieces head to tail.
  • Three conefuls. r = 7 cm, h = 24 cm, π = 22/7.

What you should see: with r = 6 and h = 8, l² = 36 + 64 = 100, so l = 10 cm. The net is a sector of radius 10 cm with angle 216°. The pieces make a near-rectangle about πr = 18.84 cm long and 10 cm wide, so the curved surface area is 188.4 cm² (π = 3.14). In the last one the cylinder holds 3696 cm³ and the cone 1232 cm³, a third of it.

Worked examples

1. A birthday cap

A conical cap has base radius 5 cm and perpendicular height 12 cm. Find its slant height and the card needed for its curved surface. If it were a closed cone, what would its total surface area be? \((\pi = 3.14)\)

  1. \(l^2 = r^2 + h^2 = 25 + 144 = 169\), so \(l =\) 13 cm
  2. Curved surface area \(= \pi rl = 3.14 \times 5 \times 13 =\) 204.1 cm²
  3. Total surface area \(= \pi r(l + r) = 3.14 \times 5 \times 18 =\) 282.6 cm²

2. A heap of grain

Grain is piled in a cone with base radius 7 m and height 24 m. Find the volume of the grain and the slant height of the heap. \((\pi = \tfrac{22}{7})\)

  1. Volume \(= \tfrac13 \pi r^2 h = \tfrac13 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 \times 24 = 22 \times 7 \times 8 =\) 1232 m³
  2. \(l^2 = 7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625\), so \(l =\) 25 m

A cylinder with the same base and height would hold \(3 \times 1232 = 3696\) m³.

3. Finding the height

The volume of a cone is 1570 cm³ and the radius of its base is 10 cm. Find its height. \((\pi = 3.14)\)

  1. \(\tfrac13 \times 3.14 \times 10^2 \times h = 1570\)
  2. \(314h = 1570 \times 3 = 4710\), so \(h = 4710 \div 314 =\) 15 cm

Common mistakes

  • Using h for the curved surface. Curved surface area is πrl, with the slant height l. Find l first from l² = r² + h².
  • Using l for the volume. Volume is ⅓πr²h, with the perpendicular height h.
  • Forgetting the ⅓. A cone holds only a third of the cylinder with the same base and height.
  • Adding the base to an open cone. A cap or a funnel has no base: use πrl only. A solid cone has the base too: πr(l + r).

Quick check

1. A cone has radius 8 cm and height 6 cm. Its slant height is…

l² = 64 + 36 = 100, so l = 10 cm.

2. A cone has radius 7 cm and slant height 10 cm. Its curved surface area (π = 22/7) is…

πrl = 22/7 × 7 × 10 = 220 cm². (374 cm² is the total surface area.)

3. A cylinder holds 900 cm³. A cone with the same base and height holds…

Three conefuls fill the cylinder, so the cone holds 900 ÷ 3 = 300 cm³.

4. The total surface area of a solid cone is…

Curved surface πrl plus the circular base πr².
