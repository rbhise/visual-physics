Work

Holding a heavy bag still for an hour is tiring, yet a physicist says you did no work at all. In science, work has a precise meaning.

Download slides (.pptx) 12 slides with teacher notes

What counts as work

Work is done when a force applied on an object causes displacement of the object. Two things are needed: a force, and movement caused by it.

  • Pushing a cart along a road: force and movement, so work is done.
  • Pushing hard against a wall that does not move: no displacement, so no work.
  • Holding a bag still: you get tired, but the bag does not move, so no work is done on it.

How much work

When the force acts in the direction of the displacement:

$$W = F \times s$$

The unit of work is the joule (J). If a force of 1 newton moves an object through 1 metre in the direction of the force, the work done is 1 joule: \(1\ \text{J} = 1\ \text{N} \times 1\ \text{m}\).

A force at an angle

When the force acts at an angle \(\theta\) to the displacement, only the part of the force along the motion does work. That part is \(F\cos\theta\), so

$$W = F s \cos\theta$$

In the simulation, the coral arrow is the force and the dashed teal arrow is the part along the motion. Useful values: \(\cos 0^\circ = 1\), \(\cos 60^\circ = 0.5\), \(\cos 90^\circ = 0\), \(\cos 180^\circ = -1\).

Positive, negative and zero work

Positive (θ = 0°)

Force and displacement in the same direction. Examples: pulling a trolley forward; gravity on a falling ball.

Negative (θ = 180°)

Force opposite to the displacement. Examples: friction on a sliding box; brakes on a moving bicycle; gravity on a ball thrown upward.

Zero (θ = 90°)

Force at right angles to the displacement, or no displacement. Example: carrying a bag while walking on level ground; your upward force on the bag does no work.

Try it

Each set-up uses a 50 N force and moves the box 6 m. Watch the work bar.

  • Pull along the floor. θ = 0°.
  • Pull at an angle. θ = 60°.
  • Force at right angles. θ = 90°.
  • Force against the motion. θ = 180°.

What you should see: pulling straight along gives 50 × 6 = 300 J. At 60°, only half the force acts along the motion, so the work is 150 J. At 90° the work is zero, even though the force is just as big. At 180° the work is −300 J.

Worked examples

1. Pushing a handcart

A vendor pushes a handcart with a force of 60 N along the road, moving it 5 m in the direction of the push. How much work does he do?

  1. \(W = F \times s = 60 \times 5 = \) 300 J

2. Pulling a suitcase

A traveller pulls a suitcase with a force of 100 N along a handle that makes 60° with the floor. The suitcase moves 10 m. Find the work done.

  1. \(W = F s \cos\theta = 100 \times 10 \times \cos 60^\circ\)
  2. \(= 100 \times 10 \times 0.5 = \) 500 J

Pulling at an angle wastes part of the force: only 50 N of it moves the suitcase forward.

3. Lifting a school bag

A student lifts a 5 kg school bag from the floor onto a table 0.8 m high at a steady speed. Find the work done by the student and by gravity. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

  1. The lifting force equals the bag's weight: \(F = mg = 5 \times 9.8 = 49\) N, upward.
  2. Work by the student \(= 49 \times 0.8 = \) 39.2 J (positive)
  3. Gravity acts downward while the bag moves up, so its work is −39.2 J

Common mistakes

  • “I am tired, so I did work.” In physics, work needs displacement. Holding or pushing something that does not move is zero work.
  • Using the whole force at an angle. Only F cos θ, the part along the motion, does work.
  • Dropping the sign. A force opposite to the motion does negative work. Keep the minus sign.
  • Mass instead of weight. To lift something you need a force of mg newtons, not m kilograms.

Quick check

1. In which case is work done on the object?

Only the box is displaced by the applied force.

2. A force of 20 N moves a crate 3 m in the direction of the force. The work done is…

\(W = 20 \times 3 = 60\) J.

3. A coolie carries a load on his head and walks 50 m on level ground. The work done by his upward force on the load is…

The upward force is at 90° to the horizontal displacement, so \(\cos 90^\circ = 0\).

4. Friction acting on a sliding box does…

Friction acts opposite to the motion, so θ = 180° and the work is negative.
