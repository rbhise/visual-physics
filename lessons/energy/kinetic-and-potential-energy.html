Kinetic and Potential Energy

A cricket ball hit for six, water stored behind a dam, a stretched catapult: each can do work. That ability is energy.

Energy

The capacity of a body to do work is called its energy. Energy is measured in the same unit as work: the joule (J). It comes in many forms: mechanical, heat, light, sound, chemical, electrical and nuclear. This lesson looks at the two forms of mechanical energy.

Kinetic energy: energy of motion

A moving object can do work when it hits something. A moving ball pushes into sand; a moving hammer drives in a nail. The energy an object has because of its motion is its kinetic energy.

$$\text{K.E.} = \tfrac12 m v^2$$

Where it comes from: push an object of mass \(m\) from rest with a force \(F\) through a distance \(s\). The work done is \(W = Fs = (ma)s\). From \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\) with \(u = 0\), \(s = v^2/2a\). So \(W = ma \times v^2/2a = \tfrac12 mv^2\). All that work is stored as kinetic energy.

Speed matters most. Double the mass and the kinetic energy doubles. Double the speed and it becomes four times as large, because speed is squared.

Potential energy: energy of position

A raised object can do work when it falls. The energy an object has because of its position or shape is its potential energy. A stretched rubber band, a wound-up spring and a drawn bow all store potential energy in their shape.

To lift a mass \(m\) through a height \(h\) at steady speed, you push up with a force equal to its weight, \(mg\). The work done, force × height, is stored as gravitational potential energy:

$$\text{P.E.} = mgh$$

The potential energy depends only on the height, not on the path taken to get there: carrying a bag up a ramp or up a ladder to the same height stores the same energy.

Try it

The dent in the sand shows how much energy the ball brings. A bigger dent means more energy.

  • Drop from 1 m. A 2 kg ball.
  • Drop from 4 m. Same ball, four times as high.
  • Roll at 3 m/s. A 2 kg ball.
  • Roll at 6 m/s. Same ball, twice as fast.

What you should see: from 1 m the ball has 19.6 J; from 4 m it has 78.4 J, four times as much, and the dent is four times as deep. Rolling at 3 m/s it has 9 J; at 6 m/s it has 36 J. Doubling the speed gives four times the energy.

Worked examples

1. A rolling ball

Find the kinetic energy of a 0.5 kg ball rolling at 4 m/s.

  1. \(\text{K.E.} = \tfrac12 \times 0.5 \times 4^2 = \tfrac12 \times 0.5 \times 16 = \) 4 J

2. A car slowing down

A 1500 kg car travels at 20 m/s. Find its kinetic energy. What is its kinetic energy if it slows to 10 m/s?

  1. At 20 m/s: \(\tfrac12 \times 1500 \times 400 = \) 300 000 J (300 kJ)
  2. At 10 m/s: \(\tfrac12 \times 1500 \times 100 = \) 75 000 J (75 kJ)

Halving the speed leaves only a quarter of the kinetic energy.

3. Water on the roof

A water tank on a roof holds 1000 kg of water, 12 m above the ground. How much potential energy does the water have? (g = 9.8 m/s²)

  1. \(\text{P.E.} = mgh = 1000 \times 9.8 \times 12 = \) 117 600 J

This stored energy is why water flows out of the taps downstairs without a pump.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to square v. K.E. = ½mv². At 4 m/s, use 16, not 4.
  • Grams instead of kilograms. Convert to kg before using either formula: 500 g = 0.5 kg.
  • Thinking the path matters for P.E. Only the height changes the potential energy, not the route.
  • Doubling speed doubles K.E. Doubling speed makes K.E. four times as large.

Quick check

1. A 2 kg ball moves at 3 m/s. Its kinetic energy is…

\(\tfrac12 \times 2 \times 9 = 9\) J.

2. If the speed of a moving object is doubled, its kinetic energy becomes…

K.E. depends on \(v^2\), and \(2^2 = 4\).

3. A 5 kg box is placed on a shelf 2 m high. Its potential energy is (g = 9.8 m/s²)…

\(mgh = 5 \times 9.8 \times 2 = 98\) J.

4. Which of these has potential energy because of its shape?

Stretching changes its shape and stores energy in it.
