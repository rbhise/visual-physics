Power

The rate of doing work

Power is the rate at which work is done, or the rate at which energy is transferred:

$$P = \frac{W}{t}$$

The SI unit of power is the watt (W): 1 watt is 1 joule of work done in 1 second, \(1\ \text{W} = 1\ \text{J/s}\).

  • 1 kilowatt (kW) = 1000 W
  • 1 horsepower (hp) = 746 W, a unit still used for engines and pumps

In the simulation, both hoists lift their loads through the same height. The work depends on the load and the height; the power also depends on how quickly the lift happens.

The commercial unit of energy

The joule is too small for household electricity. Electricity boards charge by the kilowatt hour (kWh), the "unit" on your bill: the energy used by a 1 kW appliance running for 1 hour.

$$1\ \text{kWh} = 1000\ \text{W} \times 3600\ \text{s} = 3.6 \times 10^6\ \text{J}$$

Energy in kWh = power in kW × time in hours.

Try it

Both hoists lift through 10 m. Compare their work and power.

  • Same load, different times. 50 kg each; A takes 10 s, B takes 20 s.
  • Different loads, same time. A lifts 40 kg, B lifts 80 kg, both in 10 s.
  • Equal power. A lifts 40 kg in 10 s; B lifts 80 kg in 20 s.

What you should see: with the same load, both do 4900 J of work, but A finishes in half the time, so A's power (490 W) is twice B's (245 W). With different loads in the same time, B does twice the work and has twice the power. In the last case B does twice the work in twice the time, so both develop 392 W.

Worked examples

1. Climbing the stairs

Raju, whose mass is 50 kg, runs up a staircase 12 m high in 20 s. Find the work he does and his power. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

  1. Work = \(mgh = 50 \times 9.8 \times 12 = \) 5880 J
  2. Power = \(5880 \div 20 = \) 294 W

2. A water pump

A pump lifts 200 kg of water into a tank 15 m high in 60 s. Find its power in watts and in horsepower.

  1. Work = \(200 \times 9.8 \times 15 = 29\,400\) J
  2. Power = \(29\,400 \div 60 = \) 490 W
  3. In horsepower: \(490 \div 746 \approx \) 0.66 hp

3. The geyser bill

A 1500 W geyser runs for 40 minutes every day. How many units does it use in 30 days, and what does that cost at ₹8 per unit?

  1. Power = 1.5 kW; time per day = 40/60 h. Units per day \(= 1.5 \times \tfrac{2}{3} = 1\) kWh.
  2. In 30 days: 30 kWh
  3. Cost \(= 30 \times 8 = \) ₹240

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up work and power. Work is in joules. Power is joules per second, in watts.
  • Minutes instead of seconds. In P = W/t, use seconds to get watts. For kWh, use hours.
  • Watts instead of kilowatts. For kWh, divide watts by 1000 first: 1500 W = 1.5 kW.
  • “A kWh is a unit of power.” The kilowatt hour is a unit of energy: power × time.

Quick check

1. A motor does 6000 J of work in 30 s. Its power is…

\(P = 6000 / 30 = 200\) W.

2. How many joules are there in 1 kWh?

\(1000\ \text{W} \times 3600\ \text{s} = 3.6 \times 10^6\) J.

3. 1 horsepower is equal to…

1 hp = 746 W.

4. Two girls of equal mass climb the same stairs. One takes 10 s, the other 15 s. Which statement is true?

The work is the same, but the faster girl does it in less time, so her power is greater.
