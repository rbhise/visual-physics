Conservation of Energy

Energy never appears from nothing and never vanishes. It only changes form. Watch it happen as a ball falls, a pendulum swings and a child slides.

Energy changes form

Energy is constantly being transformed from one form into another:

  • A solar panel turns light energy into electrical energy.
  • A battery turns chemical energy into electrical energy; a fan turns that into kinetic energy.
  • At a hydroelectric dam, the potential energy of stored water becomes kinetic energy, then electrical energy.
  • Plants turn light energy into chemical energy stored in food.

The law of conservation of energy

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be converted from one form into another. The total energy stays the same.

Free fall: following the energy

Drop a ball of mass \(m\) from height \(h\). Ignore air resistance. Look at three points:

  • A, at the top: the ball is at rest. P.E. = \(mgh\), K.E. = 0. Total = \(mgh\).
  • B, after falling a distance \(x\): P.E. = \(mg(h - x)\). Its speed satisfies \(v^2 = 2gx\), so K.E. = \(\tfrac12 m (2gx) = mgx\). Total = \(mg(h - x) + mgx = mgh\).
  • C, just before hitting the ground: P.E. = 0. Now \(v^2 = 2gh\), so K.E. = \(mgh\). Total = \(mgh\).

The potential energy lost is exactly the kinetic energy gained. In the simulation, the total bar never changes.

Pendulums and slides

A swinging pendulum keeps trading the two forms: all potential energy at the ends of the swing, all kinetic energy at the bottom. A child on a smooth slide reaches the bottom with speed \(v = \sqrt{2gh}\), whatever the slide's shape and whatever the child's mass, because \(\tfrac12 mv^2 = mgh\) and the \(m\) cancels.

What about friction? A real pendulum slowly stops. Its mechanical energy is not destroyed: friction and air resistance turn it into heat and sound. The total energy is still conserved.

Try it

Watch the three energy bars. The total bar should stay the same height.

  • Free fall. A 2 kg ball dropped from 5 m.
  • Pendulum. Released 2 m above its lowest point.
  • Light child on a slide. 1 kg from 4 m.
  • Heavy child on the slide. 5 kg from 4 m.

What you should see: in free fall the total stays at 98 J while potential energy drains into kinetic energy; the ball lands at about 9.9 m/s. The pendulum swings back and forth with the bars trading places. On the slide, both the 1 kg and the 5 kg child reach the bottom at 8.85 m/s; the heavier child simply has five times the energy.

Worked examples

1. A ball in free fall

A 2 kg ball is dropped from a height of 5 m. Find its potential and kinetic energy at the top, halfway down and just before it lands. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

  1. Top: P.E. \(= 2 \times 9.8 \times 5 = 98\) J, K.E. = 0. Total 98 J
  2. Halfway (2.5 m): P.E. \(= 49\) J, K.E. \(= 98 - 49 = 49\) J. Total 98 J
  3. Just before landing: P.E. = 0, K.E. = 98 J, so \(v = \sqrt{2 \times 98 / 2} \approx 9.9\) m/s

2. A playground slide

A child starts at rest at the top of a smooth slide 2.5 m high. How fast is she going at the bottom?

  1. Potential energy lost = kinetic energy gained: \(mgh = \tfrac12 mv^2\)
  2. \(v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 2.5} = \sqrt{49} = \) 7 m/s

Her mass cancels out, so an adult on the same smooth slide would also reach 7 m/s.

3. A swinging pendulum

A pendulum bob is pulled aside so that it rises 0.2 m above its lowest point, then released. Find its speed at the lowest point.

  1. \(v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 0.2} = \sqrt{3.92} \approx \) 1.98 m/s

Common mistakes

  • “Energy is used up.” Energy changes form; it is never destroyed. “Used up” means it turned into a form we cannot easily use, like heat.
  • Heavier objects slide faster. Without friction, speed at the bottom is √(2gh), the same for any mass.
  • Adding the same energy twice. At any point, P.E. + K.E. equals the starting energy. Do not add the starting P.E. again.
  • Ignoring units of height. Use metres in mgh. A 50 cm height is 0.5 m.

Quick check

1. A ball is dropped from a height. Just before it hits the ground, its energy is mostly…

All the potential energy it had at the top has turned into kinetic energy.

2. At the highest point of its swing, a pendulum bob has…

It stops for an instant, so K.E. = 0 and all its energy is potential.

3. A 1 kg ball falls from 10 m. Its kinetic energy just before landing is (g = 9.8 m/s²)…

K.E. = P.E. lost = \(1 \times 9.8 \times 10 = 98\) J.

4. A real pendulum slowly stops swinging. Its energy has…

Air resistance and friction convert mechanical energy into heat and sound. Total energy is conserved.
