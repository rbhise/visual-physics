Work and Energy, lesson 5

Simple Machines

With a long enough crowbar you can lift a rock you could never pick up by hand. Simple machines do not create energy; they trade force for distance.

What a simple machine does

A simple machine makes a task easier by changing the size or the direction of the force you need. The force you apply is the effort; the weight or resistance you overcome is the load.

$$\text{Mechanical advantage (MA)} = \frac{\text{load}}{\text{effort}}$$

An MA of 4 means the machine lets you move a load four times heavier than your effort. But there is no free energy: work out can never be more than work in. With less force you must push through a longer distance. In the simulation, the work by the effort always equals the work on the load (we ignore friction).

Levers

A lever is a rigid bar that turns about a fixed point, the fulcrum. The distance from the fulcrum to the load is the load arm; to the effort, the effort arm. A lever balances when

$$\text{effort} \times \text{effort arm} = \text{load} \times \text{load arm}$$

so \(\text{MA} = \dfrac{\text{effort arm}}{\text{load arm}}\). A long effort arm gives a big advantage.

Three classes of lever

Class I

Fulcrum between effort and load. Seesaw, crowbar, scissors. MA can be more or less than 1.

Class II

Load between fulcrum and effort. Wheelbarrow, nutcracker, bottle opener. MA is always more than 1.

Class III

Effort between fulcrum and load. Tongs, tweezers, your forearm. MA is less than 1, but the load moves farther and faster.

Inclined planes and pulleys

An inclined plane (ramp) of length \(L\) rising to height \(h\) lets you push a load up instead of lifting it. Without friction, effort × \(L\) = load × \(h\), so \(\text{MA} = L/h\). A longer, gentler ramp needs less force.

A single fixed pulley, like the one on a well, has MA = 1: it does not reduce the force but lets you pull down instead of lifting up. A movable pulley that moves with the load shares the weight between two strands of rope, giving an ideal MA of 2.

Try it

Each set-up shows a real tool. Compare the effort with the load.

  • Crowbar (class I). A 600 N rock, load arm 0.5 m, effort arm 3 m.
  • Wheelbarrow (class II). 600 N of bricks, load arm 0.5 m, handles 1.5 m from the wheel.
  • Tongs (class III). A 100 N load at 2 m, effort at 0.5 m.
  • Loading ramp. 600 N pushed up a 6 m ramp that rises 1.5 m.

What you should see: the crowbar needs only 100 N (MA 6), the wheelbarrow 200 N (MA 3). The tongs need 400 N to hold a 100 N load (MA 0.25), but the load end moves four times as far as your hand. The ramp needs 150 N instead of 600 N (MA 4), over 6 m instead of 1.5 m. Work in always equals work out.

Worked examples

1. A crowbar

A crowbar is used to lift a 600 N stone. The fulcrum is 0.2 m from the stone and 1.2 m from where the effort is applied. Find the effort and the mechanical advantage.

  1. effort × 1.2 = 600 × 0.2, so effort \(= 120 \div 1.2 = \) 100 N
  2. MA \(= 600 \div 100 = \) 6

2. A loading ramp

A shopkeeper pushes an 800 N sack up a smooth ramp 4 m long onto a platform 1 m high. Find the effort, the MA and the work done.

  1. effort × 4 = 800 × 1, so effort = 200 N
  2. MA \(= L/h = 4/1 = \) 4
  3. Work \(= 200 \times 4 = 800 \times 1 = \) 800 J, the same as lifting it straight up

3. Balancing a seesaw

A 20 kg child sits 1.5 m from the middle of a seesaw. Where must a 30 kg child sit to balance it?

  1. Weights: 196 N and 294 N. For balance, \(196 \times 1.5 = 294 \times d\).
  2. \(d = 294 \div 294 = \) 1 m from the middle, on the other side

The heavier child sits closer to the fulcrum.

Common mistakes

  • “Machines create energy.” A machine can reduce the force, but never the work. Less force means more distance.
  • Measuring arms from the end of the bar. Load arm and effort arm are measured from the fulcrum.
  • MA upside down. MA = load ÷ effort. A machine that helps you has MA greater than 1.
  • Thinking class III levers are useless. They need more effort, but they give speed and reach: think of tweezers or a fishing rod.

Quick check

1. A machine lifts a 500 N load with an effort of 125 N. Its mechanical advantage is…

MA = 500 ÷ 125 = 4.

2. In a wheelbarrow, the load lies between the wheel (fulcrum) and the handles. It is a…

Load in the middle means class II.

3. A smooth ramp is 5 m long and 1 m high. What effort pushes a 400 N box up it?

effort × 5 = 400 × 1, so effort = 80 N.

4. Which statement about simple machines is true?

They trade force for distance. The work stays the same (or more, with friction).
