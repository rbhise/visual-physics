Polynomials in one variable

Polynomial. An algebraic expression in which the power of the variable in every term is a whole number is called a polynomial. It is written \(p(x)\), \(q(y)\), and so on.

\(3x^2 - 5x + 1\), \(y^3 + 7\) and \(\sqrt{2}\,m - 4\) are polynomials. (The coefficients may be any real numbers; only the powers must be whole numbers.) But \(x + \dfrac{1}{x} = x + x^{-1}\) and \(\sqrt{x} + 2 = x^{\frac12} + 2\) are not polynomials.

Degree of a polynomial

Degree. The highest power of the variable in a polynomial in one variable is its degree.

\(4x^3 - x + 9\) has degree 3. A non-zero constant, such as 7, is a polynomial of degree 0 (\(7 = 7x^0\)). The zero polynomial, 0, has no degree: its degree is not defined. In a polynomial in more than one variable, the degree is the highest sum of the powers in any one term: \(3x^2y^3 + 5xy\) has degree \(2 + 3 = 5\).

Standard form, coefficient form and index form

A polynomial is in standard form when its terms are written in descending powers of the variable: \(x^4 - 2x + 5\), not \(5 - 2x + x^4\).

In coefficient form we write only the coefficients, in descending powers, putting 0 for every missing power: \(x^4 - 2x + 5\) becomes \((1, 0, 0, -2, 5)\). Going back, writing the terms with the powers of \(x\), gives the index form: \((5, 0, -1, 3)\) is \(5x^3 - x + 3\).

Value of a polynomial

To find the value of \(p(x)\) at \(x = a\), substitute \(a\) for \(x\). The result is written \(p(a)\). For \(p(x) = x^2 - 3x + 2\): \(p(2) = 4 - 6 + 2 = 0\) and \(p(-1) = 1 + 3 + 2 = 6\). On the graph, \(p(a)\) is the height of the curve above \(x = a\). When \(p(a) = 0\), the graph meets the x-axis, and \(a\) is called a zero of the polynomial.

Try it

Set the coefficients with the sliders, then drag along the graph to move x = a.

  • x³ − 4x. Find p(2).
  • 2x² − 3x + 1. Find p(1).
  • 3x − 6. Where does it meet the x-axis?
  • −2x³. Find p(−1).
  • 5. What is its degree?

What you should see: x³ − 4x is a cubic binomial with p(2) = 0; its zeroes are −2, 0 and 2. 2x² − 3x + 1 is a quadratic trinomial, p(1) = 0. 3x − 6 is linear and meets the x-axis at x = 2. −2x³ is a cubic monomial and p(−1) = −2(−1)³ = 2. The constant 5 has degree 0, and its graph never meets the x-axis.

Worked examples

1. Degree and type

State the degree and type of (i) \(7y^3 - 2y + 4\), (ii) \(5m\), (iii) \(3x^2y^3 + 5xy\).

  1. (i) Highest power 3, three terms: a cubic trinomial, degree 3.
  2. (ii) \(5m = 5m^1\), one term: a linear monomial, degree 1.
  3. (iii) Powers in \(3x^2y^3\) add to 5; in \(5xy\) to 2. Degree 5 (a binomial in two variables).

2. Coefficient form and index form

Write \(x^3 - 5x + 2\) in coefficient form, and \((2, -1, 0, 4)\) in index form.

  1. \(x^3 - 5x + 2 = 1x^3 + 0x^2 - 5x + 2\), so the coefficient form is (1, 0, −5, 2).
  2. \((2, -1, 0, 4)\) has 4 coefficients, so it starts at \(x^3\): \(2x^3 - 1x^2 + 0x + 4 = \) \(2x^3 - x^2 + 4\).

3. Values of a polynomial

For \(p(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 + 1\), find \(p(2)\) and \(p(-1)\).

  1. \(p(2) = 2(2)^3 - 3(2)^2 + 1 = 16 - 12 + 1 = \) 5
  2. \(p(-1) = 2(-1)^3 - 3(-1)^2 + 1 = -2 - 3 + 1 = \) −4

Common mistakes

  • Counting terms to get the degree. x³ − 4x has two terms but degree 3. The degree is the highest power.
  • Leaving out zeroes in coefficient form. x³ − 2 is (1, 0, 0, −2), not (1, −2). Every missing power needs a 0.
  • Sign slips with negative a. (−1)² = 1 but (−1)³ = −1. Put a negative value in brackets before you square or cube it.
  • Calling any expression a polynomial. x + 1/x and √x + 2 have powers −1 and ½, which are not whole numbers.

Quick check

1. Which of these is a polynomial?

Its powers 2, 1 and 0 are whole numbers. A coefficient like √2 is allowed.

2. The degree of \(5x^3 - x^4 + 2\) is…

The highest power of x is 4, even though that term is not written first.

3. The coefficient form of \(x^3 - 2\) is…

x³ + 0x² + 0x − 2: a 0 for each missing power.

4. If \(p(x) = x^2 - 5x + 6\), then \(p(3) = \)…

9 − 15 + 6 = 0, so 3 is a zero of p(x).
