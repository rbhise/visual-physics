Co-ordinate Geometry, lesson 1

Axes, Quadrants and Plotting Points

“Meet me at the bench two steps east and three steps north of the gate.” Two numbers fix one place. On paper, two number lines at right angles do the same job for every point of the plane.

Co-ordinates on a number line

On a number line every point has one number, its co-ordinate. Point O has co-ordinate 0. Points to the right of O have positive co-ordinates and points to the left have negative ones. If point A is 4 units to the left of O, the co-ordinate of A is \(-4\). One number is enough to fix a point on a line.

The axes and the origin

To fix a point in a plane we need two numbers. Draw a horizontal number line and a vertical number line that cross at right angles at their zero points.

The horizontal line is the X-axis and the vertical line is the Y-axis. The point where they meet is the origin, written O. On the X-axis, numbers to the right of O are positive and to the left are negative. On the Y-axis, numbers above O are positive and below O are negative.

Co-ordinates of a point

Take any point P in the plane. Draw a perpendicular from P to the X-axis and another to the Y-axis. Suppose the first one meets the X-axis at 3 and the second meets the Y-axis at 2.

Then 3 is the x co-ordinate and 2 is the y co-ordinate of P. We write P(3, 2). The x co-ordinate is always written first, so \((x, y)\) is an ordered pair. The co-ordinates of the origin are \((0, 0)\).

Order matters: \((3, 2)\) is 3 right and 2 up, while \((2, 3)\) is 2 right and 3 up. They are different points.

Quadrants

The two axes divide the plane into four parts called quadrants. They are numbered I, II, III and IV, going anticlockwise from the top right. The signs of the co-ordinates tell you the quadrant.

Points on the axes

The y co-ordinate of every point on the X-axis is 0, so such a point is \((x, 0)\). The x co-ordinate of every point on the Y-axis is 0, so such a point is \((0, y)\). A point on an axis does not lie in any quadrant.

For example, \((7, 0)\) and \((-1.5, 0)\) are on the X-axis, and \((0, -5)\) is on the Y-axis.

Plotting a point

To plot \((-4, 3)\): start at the origin. The x co-ordinate is \(-4\), so move 4 units to the left along the X-axis. The y co-ordinate is 3, so from there move 3 units up, parallel to the Y-axis. Mark the point and write its name and co-ordinates beside it.

Try it

Drag P on the grid, or use the sliders. Turn on Find the point to plot hidden points yourself.

  • Read the co-ordinates. Where do the dashed perpendiculars from P(3, 2) meet the axes?
  • Another quadrant. Which quadrant is (−4, 3) in? Which signs does it have?
  • On an axis. Where is (0, −5)? Is it in any quadrant?
  • Swap the numbers. (2, −5) and (−5, 2) use the same numbers. Are they the same point?
  • Find the point. Plot T(−3, −4) with its co-ordinates hidden, then press Check.

What you should see: P(3, 2) is 3 right and 2 up from O, in Quadrant I with signs (+, +). (−4, 3) is in Quadrant II, (−, +). (0, −5) is on the Y-axis, 5 below O, and not in any quadrant. A(2, −5) is in Quadrant IV but B(−5, 2) is in Quadrant II. For T go 3 left and 4 down: Quadrant III.

Worked examples

1. Quadrant or axis?

Without plotting, say where each point lies: \(A(-5, 2)\), \(B(4, -1)\), \(C(0, -3)\), \(D(-2.5, -4)\), \(E(6, 0)\).

  1. \(A\): \(x < 0\), \(y > 0\), signs \((-, +)\): Quadrant II
  2. \(B\): signs \((+, -)\): Quadrant IV
  3. \(C\): \(x = 0\): on the Y-axis
  4. \(D\): signs \((-, -)\): Quadrant III
  5. \(E\): \(y = 0\): on the X-axis

2. Plot and name the figure

Plot \(P(-4, -2)\), \(Q(3, -2)\), \(R(3, 2)\) and \(S(-4, 2)\). Join them in order. What figure is \(PQRS\)? How long are its sides?

  1. \(P\) and \(Q\) have the same y co-ordinate \(-2\), so \(PQ\) is horizontal. \(PQ = 3 - (-4) = 7\) units.
  2. \(Q\) and \(R\) have the same x co-ordinate 3, so \(QR\) is vertical. \(QR = 2 - (-2) = 4\) units.
  3. In the same way \(RS = 7\) and \(SP = 4\), and every corner is a right angle.
  4. \(PQRS\) is a rectangle, 7 units by 4 units

3. Reasoning with signs

The point \((a, b)\) lies in Quadrant II. In which quadrant does \((b, a)\) lie?

  1. Quadrant II has signs \((-, +)\), so \(a\) is negative and \(b\) is positive.
  2. In \((b, a)\) the x co-ordinate is \(b\) (positive) and the y co-ordinate is \(a\) (negative).
  3. Signs \((+, -)\): Quadrant IV. For example \((-3, 5)\) is in Quadrant II and \((5, -3)\) is in Quadrant IV.

Common mistakes

  • Writing y first. (x, y): the x co-ordinate always comes first. (1, 4) is 1 right and 4 up, not 4 right and 1 up.
  • Ignoring the minus sign. x = −3 means 3 units to the left of O, and y = −3 means 3 units below O.
  • Putting axis points in a quadrant. (0, 6) and (−2, 0) lie on the axes, not in any quadrant.
  • Numbering quadrants clockwise. Quadrants go anticlockwise: I top right, II top left, III bottom left, IV bottom right.

Quick check

1. In which quadrant does (−7, −2) lie?

Both co-ordinates are negative, signs (−, −): Quadrant III.

2. Where does the point (0, 4) lie?

Its x co-ordinate is 0, so it is on the Y-axis, 4 units above O.

3. A point has a positive x co-ordinate and a negative y co-ordinate. Where is it?

Signs (+, −) belong to Quadrant IV, below the X-axis and right of the Y-axis.

4. To plot (−2, 5), starting from the origin you move …

x = −2 means 2 units left along the X-axis; y = 5 means 5 units up.
