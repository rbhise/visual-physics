Co-ordinate Geometry, lesson 2

Lines Parallel to the Axes

Walk straight up a road that runs north from a point 3 km east of the gate. However far you walk, you stay 3 km east. Every point on that road has the same x co-ordinate, so one short equation describes the whole road.

A line parallel to the Y-axis

Plot \((3, -2)\), \((3, 0)\), \((3, 1)\) and \((3, 4)\). They all lie on one vertical line, 3 units to the right of the Y-axis. Every point on this line has x co-ordinate 3, whatever its y co-ordinate is. So the line is described by the equation \(x = 3\).

The equation of a line parallel to the Y-axis is \(x = a\). It is at a distance of \(|a|\) units from the Y-axis: on the right of the Y-axis if \(a\) is positive, on the left if \(a\) is negative.

A line parallel to the X-axis

In the same way, \((-6, -2)\), \((0, -2)\) and \((5, -2)\) all have y co-ordinate \(-2\). They lie on a horizontal line 2 units below the X-axis, and its equation is \(y = -2\).

The equation of a line parallel to the X-axis is \(y = b\). It is at a distance of \(|b|\) units from the X-axis: above the X-axis if \(b\) is positive, below it if \(b\) is negative.

Equations of the axes

Every point on the X-axis has y co-ordinate 0, so the equation of the X-axis is \(y = 0\). Every point on the Y-axis has x co-ordinate 0, so the equation of the Y-axis is \(x = 0\).

Recognising these lines

An equation with only \(x\) in it gives a line parallel to the Y-axis, and one with only \(y\) gives a line parallel to the X-axis. Make the variable the subject first:

  • \(x + 5 = 0\) gives \(x = -5\): parallel to the Y-axis, 5 units to its left.
  • \(2y - 7 = 0\) gives \(y = 3.5\): parallel to the X-axis, 3.5 units above it.

Where two such lines meet

The line \(x = a\) and the line \(y = b\) are perpendicular to each other. They meet at exactly one point, and that point has x co-ordinate \(a\) and y co-ordinate \(b\): the point \((a, b)\). Two different lines \(x = a\) and \(x = c\) are parallel to each other, so they never meet.

Try it

Drag either line on the grid (grab the crossing point to move both), or use the sliders.

  • Read the equations. Every point on the vertical line has the same x co-ordinate. Which?
  • Negative values. Where is x = −5? How far is it from the Y-axis?
  • The axes themselves. Set a = 0 and b = 0. Which lines do you get?
  • Rearrange first. Draw 2y − 7 = 0 together with x − 1.5 = 0.
  • Hit the target. Move the two lines so both pass through T(−4, −3).

What you should see: x = 3 is 3 units right of the Y-axis and y = −2 is 2 units below the X-axis; they meet at (3, −2). x = −5 is 5 units left of the Y-axis. With a = 0 and b = 0 the lines are the Y-axis and the X-axis, meeting at the origin. 2y − 7 = 0 is the line y = 3.5, and it meets x = 1.5 at (1.5, 3.5). For T you need x = −4 and y = −3.

Worked examples

1. A line below the X-axis

Write the equation of the line parallel to the X-axis and 4 units below it. Which of \(A(2, -4)\), \(B(-4, 2)\) and \(C(0, -4)\) lie on it?

  1. Parallel to the X-axis means \(y = b\). Below the axis means \(b\) is negative, so \(b = -4\).
  2. The equation is y = −4
  3. A point is on the line when its y co-ordinate is \(-4\): A and C lie on it; B does not (its y co-ordinate is 2).

2. Lines through a point

Write the equations of the lines through \(P(-5, 2)\) that are parallel to the axes. How far is each line from the axis it is parallel to?

  1. The line parallel to the Y-axis has every x co-ordinate equal to \(-5\): x = −5, 5 units to the left of the Y-axis.
  2. The line parallel to the X-axis has every y co-ordinate equal to 2: y = 2, 2 units above the X-axis.
  3. Check: the two lines meet at \((-5, 2)\), which is \(P\).

3. Four lines make a rectangle

Draw \(x + 3 = 0\), \(x - 5 = 0\), \(y + 1 = 0\) and \(2y - 8 = 0\). Find the points where they meet and the size of the figure they enclose.

  1. Rearrange: \(x = -3\), \(x = 5\), \(y = -1\), \(y = 4\). Two lines are parallel to the Y-axis and two are parallel to the X-axis.
  2. Each vertical line meets each horizontal line: \((-3, -1)\), \((5, -1)\), \((5, 4)\), \((-3, 4)\).
  3. Width \(= 5 - (-3) = 8\) units, height \(= 4 - (-1) = 5\) units.
  4. The lines enclose a rectangle 8 units by 5 units with those four corners.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up the directions. x = 3 is a vertical line (parallel to the Y-axis), not a line along the X-axis.
  • The wrong equation for an axis. The X-axis is y = 0 and the Y-axis is x = 0.
  • Forgetting to rearrange. x + 6 = 0 is the line x = −6, which is 6 units to the left of the Y-axis, not at 6.
  • A negative distance. x = −4 is 4 units from the Y-axis. Distance is never negative; the sign tells you the side.

Quick check

1. What is the equation of the X-axis?

Every point on the X-axis has y co-ordinate 0.

2. The line y − 6 = 0 is …

y − 6 = 0 gives y = 6: a horizontal line 6 units above the X-axis.

3. Where do the lines x = −2 and y = 7 meet?

Every point on x = −2 has x co-ordinate −2 and every point on y = 7 has y co-ordinate 7, so they meet at (−2, 7).

4. Which point lies on the line x = 4?

Its x co-ordinate is 4. The y co-ordinate can be anything.
