Neutralisation and Salts

An antacid tablet soothes an acidic stomach, and farmers add lime to sour soil. Both use the same reaction: an acid and a base cancelling each other out.

Neutralisation

When an acid reacts with a base, they cancel each other's properties. The reaction is called neutralisation, and it always makes a salt and water:

$$\text{acid} + \text{base} \rightarrow \text{salt} + \text{water}$$
$$\text{HCl} + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

What really happens is that the H⁺ ions from the acid join the OH⁻ ions from the base to make water: \(\text{H}^+ + \text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}\). The other ions (here Na⁺ and Cl⁻) stay in solution as the salt.

Titration

In a titration, a base is added drop by drop from a burette to a measured volume of acid containing an indicator. When just enough base has been added to use up all the acid, the solution is neutral and the indicator changes colour. This is the end point.

The simulation uses 20 mL of acid and a base of the same strength. Watch the pH curve: it rises slowly at first, then jumps steeply around 20 mL, then levels off again.

Salts

A salt is an ionic compound formed when the hydrogen of an acid is replaced by a metal (or by the ammonium group). The name comes from both parents: the metal from the base and the ending from the acid.

AcidBaseSalt formed
Hydrochloric acid, HClSodium hydroxide, NaOHSodium chloride, NaCl
Sulphuric acid, H₂SO₄Sodium hydroxide, NaOHSodium sulphate, Na₂SO₄
Nitric acid, HNO₃Potassium hydroxide, KOHPotassium nitrate, KNO₃
Hydrochloric acid, HClAmmonium hydroxide, NH₄OHAmmonium chloride, NH₄Cl

Salts are ionic compounds. In water they break up (dissociate) into positive and negative ions, for example \(\text{NaCl} \rightarrow \text{Na}^+ + \text{Cl}^-\). That is why salt solutions conduct electricity.

Neutralisation in daily life

  • Indigestion: antacids such as milk of magnesia (a weak base) neutralise extra stomach acid.
  • Insect stings: an ant or bee sting is acidic; rubbing baking soda solution on it neutralises the acid.
  • Soil: farmers add lime (calcium hydroxide) to soil that is too acidic.
  • Tooth decay: toothpaste is slightly basic and neutralises acid made by bacteria in the mouth.

Try it

Run each titration and watch the indicator and the pH curve.

  • HCl with phenolphthalein. When does the flask turn pink?
  • HCl with methyl orange. Red to yellow instead.
  • Sulphuric acid. Two H⁺ per molecule.
  • Nitric acid and KOH. Which salt forms?

What you should see: phenolphthalein stays colourless until just past 20 mL, then turns pink: one drop changes the pH from about 4 to about 10. Methyl orange changes from red to yellow slightly before the end point. With 0.05 M sulphuric acid the curve is the same, because each molecule gives two H⁺. Nitric acid and potassium hydroxide make potassium nitrate.

Worked examples

1. Naming the salt

Write the equation for the neutralisation of sulphuric acid by sodium hydroxide and name the salt.

  1. Each H₂SO₄ gives two H⁺, so it needs two NaOH:
  2. \(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}\); the salt is sodium sulphate

2. The end point

20 mL of 0.1 M hydrochloric acid is titrated with 0.1 M sodium hydroxide. What volume of base is needed, and how much salt forms?

  1. Acid: \(0.1 \times 20 = 2\) mmol of H⁺. Base needed: 2 mmol of OH⁻.
  2. Volume of base \(= 2 / 0.1 = \) 20 mL
  3. Salt formed: 2 mmol of NaCl \(= 0.002 \times 58.5 = 0.117\) g

3. An ant sting

Ant stings contain formic acid. Which would you put on a sting: vinegar or a paste of baking soda? Why?

  1. Vinegar is an acid and would not help.
  2. Baking soda is a weak base that neutralises the acid: baking soda paste

Common mistakes

  • “A salt means table salt.” Sodium chloride is just one salt. Any compound made by neutralisation is a salt.
  • Forgetting the water. Neutralisation always makes water as well as a salt.
  • Unbalanced sulphuric acid. H₂SO₄ has two H⁺, so it reacts with 2NaOH.
  • Expecting a gradual colour change. Near the end point the pH jumps sharply; one drop can change the colour.

Quick check

1. Neutralisation of an acid by a base produces…

Acid + base → salt + water.

2. Which salt forms when nitric acid reacts with potassium hydroxide?

The metal (potassium) comes from the base, the nitrate from the acid.

3. During a titration of HCl with NaOH using phenolphthalein, the flask turns pink when…

Phenolphthalein turns pink only in basic solution.

4. Why does a farmer add lime to acidic soil?

Lime is a base that neutralises the acid in the soil.
