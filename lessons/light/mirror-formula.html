The Mirror Formula and Magnification

Ray diagrams tell you roughly where an image is. The mirror formula tells you exactly, as long as you keep track of which way each distance is measured.

The Cartesian sign convention

Treat the principal axis as the x-axis of a graph with the pole P as the origin, and always draw the object on the left of the mirror, so light travels from left to right.

  • Distances are measured from the pole.
  • Distances to the right of P (the direction light travels) are positive; distances to the left are negative. So the object distance \(u\) is always negative.
  • Heights above the axis are positive; heights below are negative.
  • The focal length of a concave mirror is negative (F is in front, on the left); that of a convex mirror is positive (F is behind).

The mirror formula

The object distance \(u\), image distance \(v\) and focal length \(f\) are linked by

$$\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$$

Put in every value with its sign. A negative \(v\) means the image is in front of the mirror (real); a positive \(v\) means it is behind (virtual).

Magnification

Magnification \(m\) compares the height of the image \(h_2\) with the height of the object \(h_1\):

$$m = \frac{h_2}{h_1} = -\frac{v}{u}$$
  • \(m\) negative: image inverted (and real). \(m\) positive: image erect (and virtual).
  • \(|m| > 1\): magnified. \(|m| = 1\): same size. \(|m| < 1\): diminished.

Try it

The simulation shows signed values. Check that the numbers fit the formula.

  • Beyond C. Concave, f = 12 cm, object 30 cm away.
  • Inside F. Concave, f = 20 cm, object 10 cm away.
  • Convex. f = 20 cm, object 20 cm away.
  • Watch the signs. Walk the object in and watch v and m change sign.

What you should see: u is always negative. For the concave mirror, v and m are negative while the image is real, become infinite at F, and turn positive once the object is inside F. For the convex mirror v and m are always positive and m stays below 1.

Worked examples

1. A real image

A 6 cm tall candle stands 30 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 12 cm. Find the position, size and nature of the image.

  1. Signs: \(u = -30\) cm, \(f = -12\) cm.
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{u} = -\dfrac{1}{12} + \dfrac{1}{30} = \dfrac{-5 + 2}{60} = -\dfrac{3}{60}\), so \(v = \) −20 cm (20 cm in front of the mirror)
  3. \(m = -\dfrac{v}{u} = -\dfrac{-20}{-30} = -0.67\); \(h_2 = m h_1 = -0.67 \times 6 = \) −4 cm
  4. The image is real, inverted and diminished; a screen 20 cm from the mirror would show it.

2. A shaving mirror

A man holds his face 10 cm from a concave mirror of focal length 20 cm. Where is the image and how much is it magnified?

  1. \(u = -10\) cm, \(f = -20\) cm.
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{v} = -\dfrac{1}{20} + \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{-1 + 2}{20} = \dfrac{1}{20}\), so \(v = \) +20 cm (behind the mirror)
  3. \(m = -\dfrac{20}{-10} = \) +2: virtual, erect, twice as large

3. A convex mirror

A car is 20 m behind a convex rear-view mirror of focal length 20 cm. To keep the numbers simple, first find the image of an object 20 cm away, then think about the car.

  1. Object at 20 cm: \(u = -20\), \(f = +20\). \(\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{20} + \dfrac{1}{20} = \dfrac{1}{10}\), so \(v = \) +10 cm, \(m = -\dfrac{10}{-20} = +0.5\)
  2. For the car, \(u = -2000\) cm: \(\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{20} + \dfrac{1}{2000} = \dfrac{101}{2000}\), so \(v \approx\) +19.8 cm, just inside F, and \(m \approx +0.01\): a tiny upright image

4. Finding f

An object 12 cm in front of a convex mirror forms an image 6 cm behind it. Find the focal length.

  1. \(u = -12\) cm, \(v = +6\) cm.
  2. \(\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{-12} = \dfrac{2 - 1}{12} = \dfrac{1}{12}\), so \(f = \) +12 cm (positive, as it must be for a convex mirror)

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out the signs. Write u, v and f with their signs before you calculate; u is always negative.
  • Taking f positive for a concave mirror. Concave: f negative. Convex: f positive.
  • Forgetting to flip at the end. The formula gives 1/v. Turn the fraction over to get v.
  • Reading m without its sign. A negative m means the image is inverted; positive means erect.

Quick check

1. In the Cartesian sign convention, the object distance u is…

The object is on the left of the pole, against the direction of positive distances.

2. A concave mirror has f = −10 cm. An object is at u = −30 cm. The image distance v is…

1/v = −1/10 + 1/30 = −2/30, so v = −15 cm.

3. The magnification of an image is +2. The image is…

Positive m means erect (virtual); |m| > 1 means magnified.

4. An object 15 cm from a convex mirror forms an image 7.5 cm behind it. The magnification is…

m = −v/u = −(+7.5)/(−15) = +0.5.
