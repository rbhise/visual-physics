Reflection of Light, lesson 1

Reflection and Plane Mirrors

You see this page because light bounces off it and into your eyes. A mirror bounces light so neatly that it makes an image of the world behind the glass.

Light and reflection

Light is a form of energy that gives us the sensation of vision. It travels in straight lines, and we draw its path as a ray: a line with an arrow showing the direction. When light falls on a surface and bounces back, we call it reflection.

A mirror is a very smooth, shiny surface that reflects almost all the light falling on it. An ordinary mirror is a sheet of glass with a thin coat of silver or aluminium on the back. A flat mirror is called a plane mirror.

The laws of reflection

Draw a line at right angles to the mirror at the point where a ray strikes it. This line is the normal. The angle between the incoming (incident) ray and the normal is the angle of incidence, i. The angle between the reflected ray and the normal is the angle of reflection, r.

  • First law: the incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in the same plane, and the incident and reflected rays are on opposite sides of the normal.
  • Second law: the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence, \(\angle r = \angle i\).

Regular and irregular reflection

On a smooth mirror, rays that arrive parallel leave parallel. This is regular reflection, and it is why a mirror shows a clear image. On a rough surface such as paper or a wall, the normal points a different way at every tiny bump, so parallel rays scatter in many directions. This is irregular (or diffuse) reflection. Each ray still obeys \(i = r\); only the normals differ. Diffuse reflection is what lets you see a page from any angle.

The image in a plane mirror

Rays from an object reflect off the mirror and spread out towards your eye. Your brain traces them back in straight lines, so they seem to come from a point behind the mirror. The image formed by a plane mirror is:

  • virtual: the rays do not really meet there, so it cannot be caught on a screen;
  • erect (upright) and the same size as the object;
  • as far behind the mirror as the object is in front of it;
  • laterally inverted: left and right are swapped. Hold up your right hand and your image raises its left.
How tall a mirror? To see yourself from head to toe you need a mirror only half your height, hung so its top is halfway between your eyes and the top of your head. Distance does not matter.

Worked examples

1. Angle from the surface

A ray strikes a plane mirror making an angle of 25° with the mirror's surface. Find the angle of incidence, the angle of reflection, and the angle between the incident and reflected rays.

  1. The normal is at 90° to the surface, so \(i = 90° - 25° = \) 65°
  2. By the second law, \(r = i = \) 65°
  3. Angle between the rays \(= i + r = \) 130°

2. Walking towards a mirror

Asha stands 3 m in front of a plane mirror. How far is she from her image? She walks 1 m towards the mirror. How far is she from her image now?

  1. The image is 3 m behind the mirror, so the distance is \(3 + 3 = \) 6 m
  2. Now she is 2 m from the mirror and her image is 2 m behind it: 4 m

Moving 1 m closer brought her 2 m closer to her image.

3. A mirror for the hall

Rohan is 176 cm tall and his eyes are 166 cm above the floor. What is the shortest mirror in which he can see his whole body, and how high should its bottom edge be?

  1. Mirror length \(= 176 \div 2 = \) 88 cm
  2. Bottom edge: halfway between his feet and his eyes, \(166 \div 2 = \) 83 cm above the floor

Common mistakes

  • Measuring the angle from the mirror. Angles of incidence and reflection are always measured from the normal.
  • “The image is on the mirror.” It is behind the mirror, as far behind as the object is in front.
  • “Rough surfaces break the laws.” Every ray still obeys i = r; the normals just point different ways.
  • “You need a mirror as tall as you are.” Half your height is enough, at any distance.

Quick check

1. A ray hits a mirror with an angle of incidence of 40°. The angle of reflection is…

The angle of reflection always equals the angle of incidence.

2. Why can you read a book from almost any direction?

Diffuse (irregular) reflection sends light from each point of the page in many directions.

3. A candle is 15 cm in front of a plane mirror. Its image is…

A plane mirror forms a virtual image as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

4. The word AMBULANCE is written reversed on the front of an ambulance because…

A mirror swaps left and right, so the reversed word reads correctly in the mirror.
