Mean, Median and Mode

One number that describes a whole set of data is called a measure of central tendency. There are three: the mean, the median and the mode, and each tells a slightly different story.

Download slides (.pptx) 12 slides with teacher notes

Arithmetic mean

Mean \(= \dfrac{\text{sum of all the observations}}{\text{number of observations}}\). In symbols, \(\bar{x} = \dfrac{\sum x_i}{n}\).

The mean is the balance point of the data. Put a dot for each observation on a ruler: the ruler balances on a support placed exactly at the mean. The mean need not be one of the observations.

When the data is in a frequency table, multiply each observation \(x_i\) by its frequency \(f_i\), add the products, and divide by the total frequency \(N\):

$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{N}$$

Median

Arrange the observations in ascending order. The median is the middle observation: half the data lies below it and half above.

  • If \(n\) is odd, the median is the \(\left(\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right)\)th observation.
  • If \(n\) is even, the median is the mean of the \(\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\right)\)th and the \(\left(\dfrac{n}{2}+1\right)\)th observations.

Mode

The mode is the observation that occurs most often, the one with the highest frequency. A set of data can have more than one mode; if every observation occurs only once, there is no mode.

A shopkeeper ordering school shoes cares about the mode (the size most children wear). A teacher comparing two classes uses the mean. When one value is very far from the rest, the median often describes the data better than the mean.

Try it

Drag any dot to change it, or tap the line to add one. The see-saw tips until its support is at the mean.

  • Balance point. Support at 10 for the marks 12, 15, 8, 17, 15, 10, 14. Watch it move.
  • Even n. 4, 9, 11, 6, 15, 7: two middle values.
  • Two modes. 3, 5, 5, 7, 9, 9, 11.
  • One far value. 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 20: compare mean and median.
  • Frequency table. Shoe sizes of 30 students.

What you should see: the support slides from 10 to 13, the mean, and the see-saw levels. With six values the median is (7 + 9) ÷ 2 = 8. The third set has two modes, 5 and 9. The value 20 pulls the mean up to about 5.86 while the median stays at 4. For the shoe sizes, Σfᵢxᵢ = 183 and N = 30, so the mean is 6.1; the median and the mode are both 6.

Worked examples

1. Mean, median and mode

Seven students scored these marks out of 20: 12, 15, 8, 17, 15, 10, 14. Find the mean, median and mode.

  1. Mean \(= \dfrac{12 + 15 + 8 + 17 + 15 + 10 + 14}{7} = \dfrac{91}{7} = 13\).
  2. In order: 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 15, 17. \(n = 7\) is odd, so the median is the \(\tfrac{7+1}{2} = \) 4th observation, 14.
  3. 15 occurs twice, every other mark once. Mean 13, median 14, mode 15

2. Median of an even number of observations

Find the median of 4, 9, 11, 6, 15, 7.

  1. In order: 4, 6, 7, 9, 11, 15. \(n = 6\) is even.
  2. The middle observations are the \(\tfrac{6}{2} = \) 3rd and the 4th: 7 and 9.
  3. Median \(= \dfrac{7 + 9}{2} = \) 8. (Notice that 8 is not one of the observations.)

3. Mean from a frequency table

Shoe sizes of 30 students: size 4 – 3 students, size 5 – 7, size 6 – 9, size 7 – 6, size 8 – 5. Find the mean, median and mode.

  1. \(\sum f_i x_i = 4(3) + 5(7) + 6(9) + 7(6) + 8(5) = 12 + 35 + 54 + 42 + 40 = 183\); \(N = 30\).
  2. Mean \(= \dfrac{183}{30} = 6.1\).
  3. \(N = 30\) is even: the 15th and 16th observations. The cumulative frequencies are 3, 10, 19, …, so both are size 6. Median \(= 6\).
  4. Size 6 has the highest frequency, 9. Mean 6.1, median 6, mode 6

Common mistakes

  • Taking the middle without sorting. Arrange the observations in ascending order before finding the median.
  • One middle value when n is even. With an even n there are two middle observations; the median is their mean.
  • Giving the frequency as the mode. The mode is the observation that occurs most often, not the number of times it occurs.
  • Dividing by the number of rows. In a frequency table, divide Σfᵢxᵢ by N, the total frequency, not by the number of different values.

Quick check

1. What is the mean of 4, 7, 10 and 3?

(4 + 7 + 10 + 3) ÷ 4 = 24 ÷ 4 = 6.

2. What is the median of 3, 9, 5, 12, 6, 8?

In order: 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12. The 3rd and 4th are 6 and 8; (6 + 8) ÷ 2 = 7.

3. What is the mode of 2, 4, 4, 5, 7, 7, 7, 9?

7 occurs three times, more often than any other observation. (3 is its frequency, not the mode.)

4. In a frequency table, Σfᵢxᵢ = 240 and N = 40. The mean is…

Mean = Σfᵢxᵢ ÷ N = 240 ÷ 40 = 6.
