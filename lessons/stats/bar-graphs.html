Bar Graphs

A table of numbers hides its story; a bar graph shows it at a glance. The same data can be drawn in different ways, and each way answers a different question.

Graphs, axes and scale

A graph shows numerical information as a picture. It is drawn on graph paper with two perpendicular lines: the horizontal X-axis and the vertical Y-axis. In a bar graph, the groups are marked on the X-axis and the quantities on the Y-axis.

The scale tells how much one division on the Y-axis stands for, for example “1 cm = 10 students”. Choose it so that the tallest bar fits on the paper and is easy to read. The scale must start at 0 and go up in equal steps.

Rules for a bar graph. All bars have the same width, the gaps between bars are equal, and the height of each bar is proportional to the quantity it shows.

Double bar graph

When two quantities are given for each group, such as boys and girls in each division, draw two bars side by side for each group, in two colours. A double bar graph makes it easy to compare the two quantities within each group.

Subdivided bar graph

In a subdivided bar graph each group has one bar whose height is the total. The bar is divided into parts, one for each quantity: the first part starts at 0 and the second part sits on top of it.

Percentage bar graph

Groups often have different totals, so comparing raw numbers can mislead. In a percentage bar graph every bar is 100 units tall, and each part is drawn as a percentage of its own group's total:

$$\text{Percentage of a part} = \frac{\text{part}}{\text{total of the group}} \times 100$$

For example, Div A has 24 boys out of 40 students: \(\tfrac{24}{40} \times 100 = 60\%\) boys and \(40\%\) girls.

Try it

Pick the data and the form of the graph. Tap any bar to read it; drag the top of a bar to change the data.

  • Double bar graph. Boys and girls in four divisions.
  • Subdivided. The same data, one bar per division.
  • Percentage. The same data, every bar 100%.
  • Change the scale. Crop yields with 1 division = 20 quintals.

What you should see: Div B has more girls (25) than boys (20). In the subdivided graph each bar reaches the total: 40, 45, 50 and 40. In the percentage graph every bar is equally tall, and Div D has the smallest share of boys, 15 ÷ 40 × 100 = 37.5%. With 1 division = 20 quintals, the crop bars need 6 divisions to reach 120.

Worked examples

1. Choosing a scale

A farm grew rice and wheat (in quintals): 2021: 60 and 40; 2022: 75 and 45; 2023: 50 and 70; 2024: 90 and 30. Choose a scale for a subdivided bar graph.

  1. A subdivided bar shows the total, so find the totals: 100, 120, 120 and 120.
  2. The tallest bar is 120 quintals. With 1 division = 20 quintals we need \(120 \div 20 = 6\) divisions; with 1 division = 10 quintals, 12 divisions.
  3. 1 division = 10 or 20 quintals both fit; pick the one that suits the size of your graph paper.

2. Percentages for one bar

Div B has 20 boys and 25 girls. Find the percentages for its bar in a percentage bar graph.

  1. Total \(= 20 + 25 = 45\).
  2. Boys \(= \tfrac{20}{45} \times 100 \approx 44.4\%\); girls \(= \tfrac{25}{45} \times 100 \approx 55.6\%\).
  3. Check: \(44.4 + 55.6 = 100\). Boys ≈ 44.4%, girls ≈ 55.6%

3. Reading a double bar graph

A shop's double bar graph shows mangoes and bananas sold (in dozens) from Monday to Friday. On Tuesday the mango bar reaches 20 and the banana bar 60. How many more dozen bananas than mangoes were sold, and what was the total?

  1. Difference \(= 60 - 20 = 40\) dozen.
  2. Total \(= 60 + 20 = 80\) dozen.
  3. 40 dozen more bananas; 80 dozen in all

Common mistakes

  • Uneven scale. The Y-axis must start at 0 and rise in equal steps: 0, 10, 20, 30…
  • Starting the second part at 0. In a subdivided bar the second part sits on top of the first; the top of the bar is the total.
  • Dividing by the grand total. In a percentage bar graph, divide each part by its own group's total, not by the total of all groups.
  • Unequal widths or gaps. Only the height should change from bar to bar.

Quick check

1. In a percentage bar graph, the height of every bar is…

Every bar stands for 100%; only the parts inside it change.

2. A division has 18 boys and 27 girls. In a percentage bar graph, the girls' part is…

Total 45; 27 ÷ 45 × 100 = 60%.

3. Which graph shows a group's total and its parts in a single bar?

The bar is as tall as the total and is divided into the parts.

4. The largest value to plot is 84 and 1 division = 10 units. How many divisions does the Y-axis need at least?

84 ÷ 10 = 8.4, so 8 divisions are not enough; 9 divisions reach 90.
