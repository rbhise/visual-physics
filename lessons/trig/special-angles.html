Trigonometry, lesson 2

Ratios of Special Angles

Your two set squares hold the most useful values in trigonometry. One is half of an equilateral triangle, the other is half of a square, and from them you can work out the ratios of 30°, 45° and 60° exactly.

30° and 60°: half of an equilateral triangle

Take an equilateral triangle with side 2. Every angle is \(60^\circ\). The altitude from the top cuts it into two right triangles with angles \(30^\circ\), \(60^\circ\) and \(90^\circ\). In each half:

  • hypotenuse \(= 2\); the side opposite \(30^\circ\) \(= 1\) (half the base);
  • the altitude, opposite \(60^\circ\), \(= \sqrt{2^2 - 1^2} = \sqrt3\).

So \(\sin 30^\circ = \tfrac12\), \(\cos 30^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\), \(\tan 30^\circ = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\). From the \(60^\circ\) angle the opposite and adjacent sides swap: \(\sin 60^\circ = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\), \(\cos 60^\circ = \tfrac12\), \(\tan 60^\circ = \sqrt3\).

45°: half of a square

A diagonal cuts a square of side 1 into two right isosceles triangles with angles \(45^\circ\), \(45^\circ\), \(90^\circ\). The legs are 1 and 1, the hypotenuse is \(\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt2\). So \(\sin 45^\circ = \cos 45^\circ = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\) and \(\tan 45^\circ = 1\).

0° and 90°

Keep the hypotenuse equal to 1 and make the angle smaller and smaller. The opposite side shrinks to 0 and the adjacent side grows to 1. So \(\sin 0^\circ = 0\), \(\cos 0^\circ = 1\), \(\tan 0^\circ = 0\). As the angle grows to \(90^\circ\) the opposite way happens: \(\sin 90^\circ = 1\), \(\cos 90^\circ = 0\), and \(\tan 90^\circ = \tfrac{1}{0}\) is not defined.

A pattern to remember: from \(0^\circ\) to \(90^\circ\), \(\sin\theta\) rises from 0 to 1 and \(\cos\theta\) falls from 1 to 0, the same values in reverse. Every column also obeys \(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1\): for \(30^\circ\), \(\tfrac14 + \tfrac34 = 1\).

  • Check with a real triangle. ∠A = 60°, hypotenuse 8 cm.

What you should see: at 30° the sides are 1, √3 and 2, and sin 30° = 1/2. At 45° both legs are 1, so sin 45° = cos 45° = 1/√2 ≈ 0.707. At 90° the adjacent side is 0, so tan 90° would mean dividing by 0. In the 8 cm triangle the opposite side is about 6.93 cm, and sin 60° ≈ 0.866 = √3/2.

Worked examples

1. Evaluate

Find the value of \(6\sin 30^\circ - 2\tan 45^\circ\).

  1. \(\sin 30^\circ = \tfrac12\) and \(\tan 45^\circ = 1\).
  2. \(6 \times \tfrac12 - 2 \times 1 = 3 - 2 = \) 1

2. Squares of ratios

Find the value of \(2\cos^2 45^\circ + \tan^2 60^\circ\).

  1. \(\cos^2 45^\circ = \left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\right)^2 = \tfrac12\) and \(\tan^2 60^\circ = (\sqrt3)^2 = 3\).
  2. \(2 \times \tfrac12 + 3 = 1 + 3 = \) 4

3. A ladder

A 6 m ladder leans against a wall and makes an angle of \(60^\circ\) with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach, and how far is its foot from the wall?

  1. The ladder is the hypotenuse. Height \(= 6\sin 60^\circ = 6 \times \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} = 3\sqrt3 \approx\) 5.20 m
  2. Distance from the wall \(= 6\cos 60^\circ = 6 \times \tfrac12 =\) 3 m

Common mistakes

  • Swapping 30° and 60°. sin 30° = 1/2 and cos 60° = 1/2. Picture the triangle: the short side faces 30°.
  • tan 90° = 0. tan 90° is not defined: the adjacent side is 0 and you cannot divide by 0.
  • sin (30° + 60°) = sin 30° + sin 60°. No: sin 90° = 1, but 1/2 + √3/2 ≈ 1.37. Ratios do not add like that.
  • sin 45° = 1/2. sin 45° = 1/√2 ≈ 0.707. Only sin 30° is 1/2.

Quick check

1. tan 60° is…

In the half-equilateral triangle, opposite 60° is √3 and adjacent is 1.

2. sin 30° + cos 60° equals…

1/2 + 1/2 = 1.

3. Which of these is not defined?

tan 90° would need division by cos 90° = 0.

4. In a right triangle the hypotenuse is 10 cm and one angle is 30°. The side opposite 30° is…

Opposite = 10 × sin 30° = 10 × 1/2 = 5 cm.
