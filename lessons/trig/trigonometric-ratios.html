Trigonometry, lesson 1

Trigonometric Ratios

How tall is a tree you cannot climb? Measure the angle up to its top and your distance from it. In a right-angled triangle, the angle fixes the ratios of the sides, and those ratios are trigonometry.

Sides of a right-angled triangle

In \(\triangle ABC\), let \(\angle B = 90^\circ\). The side opposite the right angle, \(AC\), is the hypotenuse. For the acute angle \(\angle A\):

  • \(BC\), the side facing \(\angle A\), is the opposite side of \(\angle A\);
  • \(AB\), the side next to \(\angle A\) that is not the hypotenuse, is the adjacent side of \(\angle A\).

For \(\angle C\) the roles swap: \(AB\) is opposite \(\angle C\) and \(BC\) is adjacent to it. The hypotenuse never changes.

Sine, cosine and tangent

For an acute angle \(\theta\) of a right-angled triangle: $$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite side of } \theta}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \quad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent side of } \theta}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \quad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite side of } \theta}{\text{adjacent side of } \theta}$$

The ratios depend only on the angle, not on the size of the triangle. Two right triangles with the same acute angle have the same three angles, so they are similar: one is a scaled copy of the other, and every side is scaled by the same number. The ratios do not change.

Relations between the ratios

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(\angle A + \angle C = 90^\circ\). The side opposite \(\angle A\) is adjacent to \(\angle C\), so

$$\sin\theta = \cos(90^\circ - \theta), \qquad \cos\theta = \sin(90^\circ - \theta)$$

Dividing \(\sin\theta\) by \(\cos\theta\), the hypotenuse cancels:

$$\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta} = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \tan\theta$$

An important equation

By Pythagoras, \(\text{opposite}^2 + \text{adjacent}^2 = \text{hypotenuse}^2\). Divide every term by \(\text{hypotenuse}^2\):

$$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$$ Here \(\sin^2\theta\) means \((\sin\theta)^2\).

In the “Hypotenuse 1” view, the hypotenuse is 1, so the opposite side is \(\sin\theta\) and the adjacent side is \(\cos\theta\).

Try it

Use the sliders, or choose the angle at C to see the sides swap roles.

  • A small triangle. ∠A = 30°, hypotenuse 4 cm.
  • The same angle, bigger. ∠A = 30°, hypotenuse 10 cm.
  • Sides 3 cm and 4 cm. BC = 3, AB = 4: find all three ratios.
  • Hypotenuse 1. Is sin²θ + cos²θ always 1?

What you should see: with hypotenuse 4 cm the opposite side is 2 cm; with 10 cm it is 5 cm. Both times sin 30° = 0.5, cos 30° ≈ 0.866 and tan 30° ≈ 0.577. With sides 3 and 4 the hypotenuse is 5, so sin A = 3/5 = 0.6, cos A = 4/5 = 0.8 and tan A = 3/4 = 0.75. In the last view sin²θ + cos²θ stays exactly 1.

Worked examples

1. Ratios from the sides

In \(\triangle LMN\), \(\angle M = 90^\circ\), \(LM = 8\) and \(MN = 6\). Find \(\sin N\), \(\cos N\) and \(\tan N\).

  1. Hypotenuse \(LN = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10\).
  2. For \(\angle N\): opposite side \(= LM = 8\), adjacent side \(= MN = 6\).
  3. sin N = 8/10 = 4/5, cos N = 6/10 = 3/5, tan N = 8/6 = 4/3

In the simulation this is the triangle with BC = 8 and AB = 6, seen from A.

2. One ratio gives the others

If \(\sin\theta = \dfrac{8}{17}\), find \(\cos\theta\) and \(\tan\theta\).

  1. Draw a right triangle with opposite side \(8k\) and hypotenuse \(17k\). (Any \(k\) works; the ratios do not depend on the size.)
  2. Adjacent side \(= \sqrt{(17k)^2 - (8k)^2} = \sqrt{225k^2} = 15k\).
  3. cos θ = 15k/17k = 15/17, tan θ = 8k/15k = 8/15
  4. Check: \(\left(\tfrac{8}{17}\right)^2 + \left(\tfrac{15}{17}\right)^2 = \tfrac{64 + 225}{289} = 1\) ✓

In the simulation \(k = 1\): BC = 8 and AB = 15.

3. Complementary angles

Given \(\sin 35^\circ \approx 0.574\), find \(\cos 55^\circ\).

  1. \(35^\circ + 55^\circ = 90^\circ\), so \(\cos 55^\circ = \sin(90^\circ - 55^\circ) = \sin 35^\circ\).
  2. cos 55° ≈ 0.574

Common mistakes

  • Calling the hypotenuse the adjacent side. The hypotenuse touches the angle too, but it is always called the hypotenuse. The adjacent side is the other one.
  • Keeping “opposite” when the angle changes. Opposite and adjacent depend on which angle you look from. Swap them for the other acute angle.
  • Reading sin θ as sin × θ. sin θ is one number: a ratio that belongs to the angle θ.
  • Writing sin²θ as sin(θ²). sin²θ means (sin θ)²: find sin θ first, then square it.

Quick check

1. In △PQR, ∠Q = 90°. The side opposite ∠P is…

QR is the side that faces ∠P. PR is the hypotenuse.

2. If sin θ = 3/5, then cos θ is…

Opposite 3, hypotenuse 5, so adjacent = √(25 − 9) = 4, and cos θ = 4/5.

3. A right triangle is enlarged to twice its size. Its sin θ…

Both sides in the ratio double, so the ratio does not change.

4. sin 20° is equal to…

sin θ = cos (90° − θ), and 90° − 20° = 70°.
