Isosceles and Right Triangles

A set square, a roof truss, a ladder against a wall: special triangles are everywhere, and each has a property that makes it easy to work with.

Isosceles triangles

Isosceles triangle theorem. If two sides of a triangle are congruent, the angles opposite them are congruent. The converse is also true: if two angles are congruent, the sides opposite them are congruent.

So an equilateral triangle, with all three sides equal, has all three angles equal: \(60^\circ\) each. The apex of an isosceles triangle always lies on the perpendicular bisector of its base: every point on that line is the same distance from both ends of the base.

The 30°–60°–90° triangle

In a right triangle with angles \(30^\circ\), \(60^\circ\) and \(90^\circ\):

  • the side opposite \(30^\circ\) is half the hypotenuse;
  • the side opposite \(60^\circ\) is \(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\) times the hypotenuse.

The sides are in the ratio \(1 : \sqrt{3} : 2\).

The 45°–45°–90° triangle

Each leg is \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\) times the hypotenuse, and the sides are in the ratio \(1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}\). It is the shape of the other set square in your geometry box.

Median on the hypotenuse

A median joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. In a right-angled triangle, the median to the hypotenuse is half the hypotenuse. The midpoint of the hypotenuse is the centre of a circle through all three vertices.

Sides and angles

In any triangle, the largest side is opposite the largest angle (and the smallest side opposite the smallest angle). Also, the sum of any two sides is greater than the third side; otherwise the sides cannot meet.

Worked examples

1. Isosceles triangle

In \(\triangle ABC\), \(AB = AC\) and \(\angle A = 40^\circ\). Find \(\angle B\) and \(\angle C\).

  1. \(AB = AC\), so \(\angle B = \angle C\) (isosceles triangle theorem).
  2. \(2\angle B = 180^\circ - 40^\circ = 140^\circ\), so ∠B = ∠C = 70°

2. A 30°–60°–90° triangle

In a right triangle the hypotenuse is 10 cm and one angle is \(30^\circ\). Find the other two sides.

  1. Side opposite \(30^\circ\) \(= \tfrac12 \times 10 = \) 5 cm
  2. Side opposite \(60^\circ\) \(= \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} \times 10 = 5\sqrt3 \approx\) 8.66 cm

3. Median on the hypotenuse

In \(\triangle PQR\), \(\angle Q = 90^\circ\), \(PQ = 12\) and \(QR = 5\). \(S\) is the midpoint of \(PR\). Find \(QS\).

  1. \(PR = \sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13\).
  2. The median to the hypotenuse is half of it: \(QS = 13 \div 2 = \) 6.5

Common mistakes

  • Matching the wrong angle to a side. In the isosceles theorem, the equal angles are opposite the equal sides, at the base.
  • Halving the wrong side. In a 30°–60°–90° triangle, the side opposite 30° (the shortest) is half the hypotenuse.
  • Any three lengths make a triangle. Check that the two shorter sides add up to more than the longest.
  • Largest angle next to the longest side. The largest angle is opposite the longest side.

Quick check

1. In △PQR, PQ = PR and ∠P = 50°. Then ∠Q is…

(180° − 50°) ÷ 2 = 65°.

2. A 30°–60°–90° triangle has hypotenuse 14 cm. The side opposite 30° is…

Half the hypotenuse.

3. Which three lengths can form a triangle?

Only 3 + 4 > 5 works; in the others the two shorter sides are not longer than the third.

4. In a right triangle the hypotenuse is 15 cm. The median to the hypotenuse is…

It is half the hypotenuse.
