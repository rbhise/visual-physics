Triangles, lesson 2

Congruence of Triangles

Two triangles are congruent if one fits exactly on top of the other. You do not need to check all six sides and angles: three well-chosen parts are enough.

Congruent triangles

Triangles are congruent when their corresponding sides and corresponding angles are equal, so one can be placed exactly over the other. We write \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR\), listing the vertices in matching order: \(A \leftrightarrow P\), \(B \leftrightarrow Q\), \(C \leftrightarrow R\).

Some sets of three parts fix a triangle completely: only one triangle can be drawn with them. If two triangles share such a set, they must be congruent. These are the tests of congruence.

TestEqual parts
S-S-SAll three pairs of sides
S-A-STwo sides and the angle between them
A-S-ATwo angles and the side between them
A-A-STwo angles and a side not between them
Hypotenuse–sideIn right triangles: the hypotenuse and one other side

Parts that are not enough

S-S-A (two sides and an angle that is not between them) can fit two different triangles, so it is not a test. A-A-A fixes the shape but not the size: the triangles are similar, not congruent. The simulation draws every triangle that fits the parts you choose, so you can see this happen.

Once two triangles are shown congruent, all their remaining corresponding parts are equal too. This is often written as c.s.c.t. (corresponding sides of congruent triangles) and c.a.c.t. (corresponding angles of congruent triangles).

Try it

Pick a test and change the given parts.

  • S-A-S. How many triangles fit?
  • S-S-S. Try sides 3, 4 and 9.
  • S-S-A. Same parts, two triangles?
  • A-A-A. Same angles, different sizes.

What you should see: S-A-S always gives exactly one triangle. Sides 3, 4 and 9 cannot make a triangle at all, because 3 + 4 is less than 9. With S-S-A the side CA can swing to two places on the base, giving two different triangles. A-A-A gives triangles of any size.

Worked examples

1. Naming the test

In \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle PQR\), \(AB = PQ = 5\) cm, \(\angle B = \angle Q = 50^\circ\) and \(BC = QR = 7\) cm. Are they congruent?

  1. The equal angle lies between the two equal sides.
  2. So \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR\) by the S-A-S test

2. Using c.s.c.t.

Point \(S\) is joined to points \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\), with \(PS = RS\) and \(\angle PSQ = \angle RSQ\). Show that \(PQ = RQ\).

  1. In \(\triangle PSQ\) and \(\triangle RSQ\): \(PS = RS\) (given), \(\angle PSQ = \angle RSQ\) (given), \(SQ = SQ\) (common side).
  2. So \(\triangle PSQ \cong \triangle RSQ\) by S-A-S, and therefore PQ = RQ (c.s.c.t.)

3. Right triangles

Two right-angled triangles both have a hypotenuse of 10 cm and one other side of 6 cm. Are they congruent? Find the third side.

  1. Yes, by the hypotenuse–side test.
  2. Third side \(= \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = \sqrt{64} = \) 8 cm in both

Common mistakes

  • Using S-S-A as a test. Two sides and a non-included angle can fit two different triangles.
  • Using A-A-A as a test. Equal angles give the same shape, not the same size.
  • Mismatching the order. In △ABC ≅ △PQR, A matches P, B matches Q and C matches R. Keep that order.
  • Choosing the wrong angle for S-A-S. The angle must be the one between the two equal sides.

Quick check

1. Which set of equal parts does NOT prove two triangles congruent?

Equal angles alone allow triangles of different sizes.

2. In △ABC and △DEF, AB = DE, BC = EF and ∠B = ∠E. The test is…

∠B lies between AB and BC, and ∠E between DE and EF.

3. If △PQR ≅ △XYZ, which side equals QR?

Q matches Y and R matches Z.

4. Two right triangles have equal hypotenuses of 13 cm and one equal side of 5 cm. The third side of each is…

√(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12.
