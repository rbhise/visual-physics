Triangles, lesson 1

Angles of a Triangle

Draw any triangle, tear off its three corners and put them side by side: they always make a straight line. That one fact explains the exterior angle theorem too.

Download slides (.pptx) 12 slides with teacher notes

The angle sum

In every triangle the three interior angles add up to \(180^\circ\):

$$\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ$$

Drag any corner in the simulation: the angles change, but their sum never does. Press “Move the corners” to fit \(\angle A\) and \(\angle B\) next to \(\angle C\): together they make a straight angle.

Exterior angles

Extend side \(BC\) beyond \(C\) to a point \(D\). The angle \(\angle ACD\) between the extended side and side \(CA\) is an exterior angle of the triangle. The two angles of the triangle that are not next to it, \(\angle A\) and \(\angle B\), are its remote interior angles.

Theorem of remote interior angles. An exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of its remote interior angles: \(\angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B\).

Proof. \(\angle ACB + \angle ACD = 180^\circ\) because they form a linear pair. Also \(\angle A + \angle B + \angle ACB = 180^\circ\) (angle sum). Comparing the two, \(\angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B\).

It follows that an exterior angle is greater than each of its remote interior angles.

Try it

Use the chips, the sliders, or drag the corners A, B and C.

  • Angle sum. Watch the corners fill a straight line.
  • Exterior angle. Does ∠A + ∠B fill ∠ACD?
  • An obtuse angle. Make ∠B = 110°.
  • Ratio 5 : 6 : 7. Angles of 50°, 60° and 70°.

What you should see: the three angles always total 180°, and the copies of ∠A and ∠B line up with ∠C along a straight line. With ∠B = 45° and ∠C = 75°, ∠A = 60° and the exterior angle is 120° = 60° + 45°.

Worked examples

1. Angles in a ratio

The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 5 : 6 : 7. Find them.

  1. Let the angles be \(5x\), \(6x\) and \(7x\). Then \(18x = 180^\circ\), so \(x = 10^\circ\).
  2. The angles are 50°, 60° and 70°

2. An exterior angle

In \(\triangle PQR\), side \(QR\) is extended to \(S\). If \(\angle P = 55^\circ\) and \(\angle Q = 65^\circ\), find \(\angle PRS\) and \(\angle PRQ\).

  1. By the remote interior angles theorem, \(\angle PRS = 55^\circ + 65^\circ = \) 120°
  2. \(\angle PRQ = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = \) 60°

3. Exterior angles all round

Show that the three exterior angles of a triangle, one at each corner, add up to \(360^\circ\).

  1. Each exterior angle is \(180^\circ\) minus the interior angle beside it.
  2. Sum \(= 3 \times 180^\circ - (\angle A + \angle B + \angle C) = 540^\circ - 180^\circ = \) 360°

Common mistakes

  • Adding the wrong angles. The exterior angle equals the two angles not touching it, not the one beside it.
  • “Exterior and interior angles are equal.” The exterior angle and the interior angle beside it add up to 180°.
  • Two obtuse angles. Two obtuse angles already exceed 180°, so a triangle can have at most one.
  • Forgetting to use the ratio total. For a ratio 5 : 6 : 7, divide 180° by 5 + 6 + 7 = 18.

Quick check

1. Two angles of a triangle are 48° and 75°. The third angle is…

180° − 48° − 75° = 57°.

2. An exterior angle of a triangle is 110°. One remote interior angle is 40°. The other is…

110° = 40° + x, so x = 70°.

3. Which statement is always true?

It equals their sum, so it is bigger than either one.

4. Can a triangle have angles of 100°, 50° and 40°?

The angles must add up to exactly 180°.
