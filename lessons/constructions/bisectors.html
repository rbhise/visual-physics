Perpendicular and Angle Bisectors

Every triangle construction in this chapter uses two tools: the perpendicular bisector of a segment and the bisector of an angle. Here is what each one guarantees, and how to draw it with a compass and ruler.

The perpendicular bisector

The perpendicular bisector of a segment is the line that passes through its midpoint and is perpendicular to it.

Perpendicular bisector theorem. Every point on the perpendicular bisector of a segment is equidistant from the end points of the segment.
Converse. Every point which is equidistant from the end points of a segment lies on the perpendicular bisector of the segment.

Why it is true. Let line \(l\) be the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\), meeting it at \(M\), and let \(P\) be any point on \(l\). In \(\triangle PMA\) and \(\triangle PMB\): \(AM = BM\), \(\angle PMA = \angle PMB = 90^\circ\) and \(PM\) is common. So the triangles are congruent by the S-A-S test, and \(PA = PB\).

Constructing the perpendicular bisector

  1. Open the compass to more than half of \(AB\). With centre \(A\), draw an arc above \(AB\) and an arc below it.
  2. Keep the same opening. With centre \(B\), draw arcs that cut the first two at \(X\) and \(Y\).
  3. Draw line \(XY\). It is the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).

This works because of the converse: \(XA = XB\) and \(YA = YB\) (the same compass opening), so both \(X\) and \(Y\) lie on the perpendicular bisector. Two points fix a line.

The angle bisector

The bisector of an angle is the ray from the vertex that divides the angle into two congruent angles. The distance of a point from a line is the length of the perpendicular drawn from the point to the line.

Angle bisector theorem. Every point on the bisector of an angle is equidistant from the sides of the angle.
Converse. Any point equidistant from the sides of an angle lies on the bisector of the angle.

Why it is true. Let \(P\) be on the bisector of \(\angle ABC\), with \(PM \perp BA\) and \(PN \perp BC\). In \(\triangle PMB\) and \(\triangle PNB\): \(\angle PBM = \angle PBN\), \(\angle PMB = \angle PNB = 90^\circ\) and \(PB\) is common. They are congruent by the A-A-S test, so \(PM = PN\).

Constructing the angle bisector

  1. With centre \(B\) and any radius, draw an arc that cuts ray \(BA\) at \(D\) and ray \(BC\) at \(E\).
  2. With centres \(D\) and \(E\) and the same radius (more than half of \(DE\)), draw arcs that meet at \(F\).
  3. Draw ray \(BF\). It bisects \(\angle ABC\).

\(BD = BE\) and \(DF = EF\), and \(BF\) is common, so \(\triangle BDF \cong \triangle BEF\) (S-S-S) and \(\angle DBF = \angle EBF\).

Worked examples

1. Using the perpendicular bisector theorem

\(AB = 8\) cm. Point \(P\) is on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\), which meets \(AB\) at \(M\), and \(PM = 3\) cm. Find \(PA\) and \(PB\).

  1. \(M\) is the midpoint, so \(AM = 4\) cm, and \(\angle PMA = 90^\circ\).
  2. By Pythagoras, \(PA = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5\) cm.
  3. P is on the perpendicular bisector, so \(PB = PA\): PA = PB = 5 cm

2. Using the angle bisector theorem

Ray \(BF\) bisects \(\angle ABC = 60^\circ\). \(P\) is on \(BF\) and \(BP = 5\) cm. Find \(\angle PBC\) and the distance of \(P\) from each arm.

  1. \(\angle PBC = 60^\circ \div 2 = \) 30°
  2. Draw \(PN \perp BC\). \(\triangle PNB\) is a 30°–60°–90° triangle with hypotenuse \(BP\), so \(PN = \tfrac12 \times 5 = 2.5\) cm.
  3. By the angle bisector theorem, P is the same distance from \(BA\): 2.5 cm from each arm

3. Using the converse

Two villages A and B are 6 km apart. A water tank must be the same distance from both, and 4 km from each. Where can it be built? (Scale: 1 cm for 1 km.)

  1. The tank T has \(TA = TB\), so by the converse it lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\).
  2. If the bisector meets \(AB\) at \(M\), then \(AM = 3\) and \(TM = \sqrt{4^2 - 3^2} = \sqrt7 \approx 2.65\) km.
  3. On the perpendicular bisector, about 2.65 km from AB, on either side of it: two places.

Common mistakes

  • Compass opening too small. For the perpendicular bisector the radius must be more than half of AB, or the arcs from A and B never meet.
  • Changing the opening between arcs. The arcs from A and from B (or from D and from E) must have the same radius.
  • Measuring a slanting distance. The distance of a point from an arm is along the perpendicular to that arm, not along any line you like.
  • Forgetting the converse. PA = PB tells you that P is on the perpendicular bisector. You do not need to be told it separately.

Quick check

1. P is on the perpendicular bisector of segment XY and PX = 7 cm. Then PY is…

Every point on the perpendicular bisector is equidistant from X and Y.

2. QA = QB = 5 cm and AB = 8 cm. How far is Q from segment AB?

Q is on the perpendicular bisector (converse). Half of AB is 4, so the distance is √(25 − 16) = 3 cm.

3. Ray BD bisects ∠ABC = 84°. Then ∠ABD is…

A bisector divides the angle into two equal angles: 84° ÷ 2 = 42°.

4. A point inside ∠ABC is 3 cm from arm BA and 3 cm from arm BC. It lies…

Converse of the angle bisector theorem: a point equidistant from the sides is on the bisector.
