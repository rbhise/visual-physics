Constructions of Triangles, lesson 2

Constructing Triangles

You can already draw a triangle from three sides, or two sides and an angle. But what if you know only the sum of two sides, their difference, or the perimeter? A perpendicular bisector turns each of these into a construction you can do with a compass and ruler.

First, a rough figure

Before each construction, draw a rough figure and mark the given measures on it. Then ask: which point can I draw straight away, and which point do I have to find? In all three constructions below, the missing vertex is found with a perpendicular bisector, using the theorem from the last lesson: a point on the perpendicular bisector of a segment is equidistant from its end points.

1. Base, a base angle and the sum of the other two sides

Given \(BC\), \(\angle B\) and \(AB + AC\).

  1. Draw \(BC\). At \(B\) draw ray \(BX\) with the given \(\angle XBC\).
  2. Mark \(D\) on ray \(BX\) with \(BD = AB + AC\). Join \(DC\).
  3. Draw the perpendicular bisector of \(DC\). It meets \(BD\) at \(A\). Join \(AC\).
Why it works. \(A\) is on the perpendicular bisector of \(DC\), so \(AD = AC\). Then \(AB + AC = AB + AD = BD\), which is the given sum.

The triangle exists only if \(AB + AC > BC\): the sum of two sides of a triangle is greater than the third side.

2. Base, a base angle and the difference of the other two sides

Given \(BC\), \(\angle B\) and the difference of \(AB\) and \(AC\). There are two cases.

  • \(AB - AC\) is given (AB is longer). Mark \(D\) on ray \(BX\) with \(BD = AB - AC\). The perpendicular bisector of \(DC\) meets ray \(BX\) at \(A\). Since \(AD = AC\), \(AB - AC = AB - AD = BD\).
  • \(AC - AB\) is given (AC is longer). Mark \(D\) on the ray opposite to \(BX\) with \(BD = AC - AB\). The perpendicular bisector of \(DC\) meets ray \(BX\) at \(A\). Now \(AC = AD = AB + BD\), so \(AC - AB = BD\).

Here the difference must be less than \(BC\), because the difference of two sides of a triangle is less than the third side.

3. Perimeter and both base angles

Given \(\angle B\), \(\angle C\) and \(AB + BC + CA\).

  1. Draw segment \(XY\) equal to the perimeter.
  2. At \(X\) draw an angle of \(\tfrac12\angle B\), and at \(Y\) an angle of \(\tfrac12\angle C\), on the same side. The rays meet at \(A\).
  3. The perpendicular bisectors of \(AX\) and \(AY\) meet \(XY\) at \(B\) and \(C\). Join \(AB\) and \(AC\).
Why it works. \(B\) is on the perpendicular bisector of \(AX\), so \(BA = BX\) and \(\triangle ABX\) is isosceles: \(\angle BAX = \angle BXA = \tfrac12\angle B\). \(\angle ABC\) is an exterior angle of \(\triangle ABX\), so it equals \(\tfrac12\angle B + \tfrac12\angle B = \angle B\). In the same way \(CA = CY\). So \(AB + BC + CA = XB + BC + CY = XY\), the perimeter.

Try it

Choose what is given, set the measures, and press “Play construction” or “Next step”.

  • Sum. BC = 8 cm, ∠B = 60°, AB + AC = 12 cm.
  • Difference, AC longer. BC = 8 cm, ∠B = 60°, AC − AB = 2 cm.
  • Perimeter. ∠B = 70°, ∠C = 50°, perimeter 12 cm.
  • Impossible. BC = 7 cm, ∠B = 50°, AB + AC = 6 cm.

What you should see: the first two give the same triangle: AB = 5 cm and AC = 7 cm (5 + 7 = 12 and 7 − 5 = 2). With the perimeter 12 cm the angles at X and Y are 35° and 25°, and the sides come out about 3.57 cm, 4.04 cm and 4.38 cm. The last one has no triangle, because 6 is not more than 7.

Worked examples

1. Given the sum of two sides

Construct \(\triangle ABC\) with \(BC = 8\) cm, \(\angle B = 60^\circ\) and \(AB + AC = 12\) cm. Measure \(AB\) and \(AC\).

  1. Draw \(BC = 8\) cm and ray \(BX\) with \(\angle XBC = 60^\circ\). Mark \(D\) on \(BX\) with \(BD = 12\) cm. Join \(DC\).
  2. The perpendicular bisector of \(DC\) meets \(BD\) at \(A\). Join \(AC\).
  3. Measure: AB = 5 cm, AC = 7 cm, and 5 + 7 = 12 ✓

2. Given the difference of two sides

Construct \(\triangle ABC\) with \(BC = 5\) cm, \(\angle B = 60^\circ\) and \(AB - AC = 1\) cm.

  1. AB is the longer side, so mark \(D\) on ray \(BX\) itself, with \(BD = 1\) cm. Join \(DC\).
  2. The perpendicular bisector of \(DC\) meets ray \(BX\) at \(A\). Join \(AC\).
  3. Measure: AB = 8 cm, AC = 7 cm, and 8 − 7 = 1 ✓

3. Given the perimeter

Construct \(\triangle ABC\) with \(\angle B = 60^\circ\), \(\angle C = 60^\circ\) and perimeter 15 cm. What kind of triangle is it?

  1. Draw \(XY = 15\) cm. At \(X\) draw \(30^\circ\) (half of 60°) and at \(Y\) draw \(30^\circ\). The rays meet at \(A\).
  2. Perpendicular bisectors of \(AX\) and \(AY\) give \(B\) and \(C\) on \(XY\).
  3. \(\angle A = 180^\circ - 60^\circ - 60^\circ = 60^\circ\): equilateral, each side 5 cm

Common mistakes

  • Marking the sum on the base. BD = AB + AC is marked on the ray at the given angle, not along BC.
  • Wrong ray for AC − AB. When AC is the longer side, D goes on the ray opposite to BX, below the base.
  • Using the full base angles at X and Y. In the perimeter construction you draw half of ∠B and half of ∠C.
  • Not checking first. AB + AC must be more than BC, and a difference must be less than BC, or no triangle exists.

Quick check

1. To construct △PQR with QR, ∠Q and PQ + PR given, the point D is marked so that QD equals…

D is on the ray at the given angle, with QD equal to the given sum.

2. In that construction, why is PD = PR?

Every point on the perpendicular bisector of DR is equidistant from D and R.

3. A triangle has ∠B = 80° and ∠C = 50°. In the perimeter construction, the angles drawn at X and Y are…

Half of each base angle: 80° ÷ 2 = 40° and 50° ÷ 2 = 25°.

4. BC = 6 cm, ∠B = 45° and AB + AC = 5 cm. The triangle…

AB + AC must be greater than BC, and 5 is less than 6.
