Equations of Motion

Three short equations connect starting velocity, final velocity, acceleration, time and distance. All three come straight out of one velocity–time graph.

Download slides (.pptx) 12 slides with teacher notes

The five quantities

For an object moving in a straight line with uniform acceleration:

  • \(u\) = initial velocity, \(v\) = final velocity (m/s)
  • \(a\) = acceleration (m/s²), \(t\) = time taken (s)
  • \(s\) = displacement, the distance travelled in a straight line (m)

The simulation shows the velocity–time graph for these quantities. Change \(u\), \(a\) and \(t\), and every equation updates in the readout.

1. Velocity and time: \(v = u + at\)

The slope of a velocity–time graph is the acceleration. The line rises from \(u\) to \(v\) in time \(t\), so

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v = u + at$$

2. Displacement and time: \(s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2\)

The area under the graph is the displacement. Split it into two shapes:

  • a rectangle of height \(u\) and width \(t\): area \(= ut\)
  • a triangle of base \(t\) and height \(v - u = at\): area \(= \tfrac12 \times t \times at = \tfrac12 at^2\)
$$s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2$$

3. Velocity and displacement: \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\)

The whole area is also a trapezium: \(s = \tfrac12 (u + v)\,t\). From the first equation, \(t = (v - u)/a\). Substituting:

$$s = \frac{(u + v)(v - u)}{2a} = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v^2 = u^2 + 2as$$

In the readout, \(v^2\) and \(u^2 + 2as\) always match.

Which equation to use

Each equation leaves out one quantity. Pick the one that leaves out the quantity you neither know nor need.

EquationConnectsLeaves out
\(v = u + at\)u, v, a, ts
\(s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2\)u, a, t, sv
\(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\)u, v, a, st

Watch for hidden values: "starts from rest" means \(u = 0\), and "comes to rest" means \(v = 0\).

Try it

Each button sets up one of the worked examples below. Check the readout against the working.

  • From rest. u = 0, a = 2 m/s², t = 6 s.
  • Already moving. u = 4 m/s, a = 1.5 m/s², t = 4 s.
  • Slowing to a stop. u = 10 m/s, a = −2 m/s².

What you should see: from rest the area is a single triangle of 36 m and v = 12 m/s. Starting at 4 m/s, the rectangle is 16 m and the triangle 12 m, giving 28 m. Slowing from 10 m/s, the object stops after 5 s having travelled 25 m.

Worked examples

1. A car pulling away

A car starts from rest and accelerates at 2 m/s² for 6 s. Find its final velocity and the distance it covers.

  1. \(u = 0,\ a = 2,\ t = 6\).
  2. \(v = u + at = 0 + 2 \times 6 = \) 12 m/s
  3. \(s = ut + \tfrac12 at^2 = 0 + \tfrac12 \times 2 \times 36 = \) 36 m

2. A cyclist going downhill

A cyclist moving at 4 m/s accelerates at 1.5 m/s² for 4 s. Find her final velocity and the distance covered, then check with the third equation.

  1. \(v = 4 + 1.5 \times 4 = \) 10 m/s
  2. \(s = 4 \times 4 + \tfrac12 \times 1.5 \times 16 = 16 + 12 = \) 28 m
  3. Check: \(u^2 + 2as = 16 + 2 \times 1.5 \times 28 = 100 = 10^2\) ✓

3. A ball rolling to a stop

A ball rolls across a rough floor at 10 m/s and decelerates uniformly at 2 m/s². How far does it roll before stopping, and how long does that take?

  1. Comes to rest, so \(v = 0\); slowing down, so \(a = -2\). Time is not given: use \(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\).
  2. \(0 = 100 + 2(-2)s \Rightarrow s = \) 25 m
  3. \(v = u + at \Rightarrow 0 = 10 - 2t \Rightarrow t = \) 5 s

Common mistakes

  • Positive a for slowing down. If the object slows down, a is negative. Put the minus sign into the equation.
  • Missing hidden zeros. “From rest” gives u = 0. “Comes to rest” or “stops” gives v = 0.
  • Using the equations for any motion. They only work when the acceleration is uniform (constant).
  • Mixing units. Use m, s, m/s and m/s² throughout. Convert km/h first.

Quick check

1. A bike starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 4 s. Its final velocity is…

\(v = u + at = 0 + 3 \times 4 = 12\) m/s.

2. Which equation would you use if the time is not given?

\(v^2 = u^2 + 2as\) is the only one without \(t\).

3. A car moving at 20 m/s brakes and comes to rest. In the equations, its final velocity v is…

“Comes to rest” means v = 0.

4. A ball starts from rest with a = 4 m/s². How far does it go in 3 s?

\(s = 0 + \tfrac12 \times 4 \times 9 = 18\) m.
