Acceleration

A scooter pulls away from a signal and a bus brakes at a stop. Both have a changing velocity. Acceleration measures how quickly that change happens.

The rate of change of velocity

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. If an object's velocity changes from an initial value \(u\) to a final value \(v\) in time \(t\):

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t}$$

Velocity is measured in m/s and time in s, so acceleration is measured in m/s per second, written m/s². An acceleration of 2 m/s² means the velocity grows by 2 m/s every second.

In the simulation, the violet arrow is the car's velocity and the coral arrow is its acceleration. The dashed step on the graph shows the change in velocity in one second, which is the acceleration.

Three kinds of acceleration

Positive

Velocity increases. The acceleration points the same way as the motion. Example: a ball rolling down a slope.

Negative

Velocity decreases. The acceleration points opposite to the motion. This is also called deceleration or retardation. Example: a bus braking.

Zero

Velocity does not change. The object moves uniformly in a straight line, or stays at rest.

Uniform acceleration means the velocity changes by equal amounts in equal intervals of time. A ball rolling down a smooth slope is a good example. On a velocity–time graph it gives a straight sloping line.

Choose a starting velocity and an acceleration, or use these set-ups. Watch the spacing of the dots and the slope of the graph.

  • Positive acceleration. Start at 2 m/s and add 2 m/s every second.
  • Negative acceleration. Start at 16 m/s and brake at 2 m/s².
  • Zero acceleration. Hold a steady 8 m/s.

What you should see: with positive acceleration the dots spread out and the graph rises; after 8 s the car reaches 18 m/s. Braking at 2 m/s² from 16 m/s, the dots bunch up and the car stops after exactly 8 s. With zero acceleration the dots are evenly spaced and the graph is flat.

Worked examples

1. A scooter at a green light

A scooter starts from rest and reaches 12 m/s in 4 s. Find its acceleration.

  1. Starting from rest: \(u = 0\), and \(v = 12\) m/s, \(t = 4\) s.
  2. \(a = \dfrac{12 - 0}{4} = \) 3 m/s²

2. A bus pulling in

A bus slows down from 20 m/s to 5 m/s in 5 s as it approaches a stop. Find its acceleration.

  1. \(u = 20\) m/s, \(v = 5\) m/s, \(t = 5\) s.
  2. \(a = \dfrac{5 - 20}{5} = \) −3 m/s²

The minus sign shows negative acceleration: the bus is decelerating at 3 m/s².

3. Units first

A car speeds up from 18 km/h to 54 km/h in 5 s. Find its acceleration in m/s².

  1. Convert using 5/18: \(u = 18 \times \tfrac{5}{18} = 5\) m/s and \(v = 54 \times \tfrac{5}{18} = 15\) m/s.
  2. \(a = \dfrac{15 - 5}{5} = \) 2 m/s²

Common mistakes

  • Using v − u the wrong way round. Always final minus initial. Swapping them flips the sign and turns speeding up into slowing down.
  • Forgetting the units are m/s². Acceleration is a change of velocity (m/s) per second, so its unit is m/s², not m/s.
  • Mixing km/h with seconds. Convert velocities to m/s before dividing by time in seconds.
  • Thinking “at rest” means “no acceleration”. A ball thrown upward is momentarily at rest at the top, but gravity is still accelerating it.

Quick check

1. A cyclist's velocity goes from 2 m/s to 8 m/s in 3 s. Her acceleration is…

\(a = (8 - 2) / 3 = 2\) m/s².

2. A train moving at a steady 72 km/h along a straight track has an acceleration of…

Its velocity does not change, so the acceleration is zero.

3. Negative acceleration means that…

Negative acceleration (deceleration) means the velocity is decreasing, for example when braking.

4. What is the SI unit of acceleration?

Change in velocity (m/s) divided by time (s) gives m/s².
