Laws of Motion, lesson 7

Uniform Circular Motion

A merry-go-round turns at a steady rate, so every horse keeps the same speed. Yet each horse's velocity changes every moment. Here is why.

Download slides (.pptx) 12 slides with teacher notes

Constant speed, changing direction

When an object moves along a circular path at constant speed, its motion is called uniform circular motion. Examples include the tip of a clock's second hand, a stone whirled on a string, and a satellite going round the Earth.

The speed stays the same, but the direction of motion changes at every point on the circle. Since velocity includes direction, the velocity keeps changing. A changing velocity means the object is accelerating, so uniform circular motion is accelerated motion.

From polygon to circle

Pick Square in the simulation. The object goes straight along each side and changes direction only at the four corners. With 8 sides it turns 8 times, and with 16 sides 16 times. A circle is like a polygon with an enormous number of tiny sides, so the direction changes at every single point.

Which way is the velocity?

At any point, the velocity points along the tangent to the circle: at right angles to the radius, in the direction of motion. Press Let go and the object leaves the circle along that tangent, in a straight line.

You have seen this: mud flying off a spinning bicycle wheel, sparks leaving a grinding wheel, and a coin sliding off a turning disc all leave along the tangent, not straight outwards.

Finding the speed

In one full round the object travels the circumference, \(2\pi r\). If one round takes time \(t\):

$$v = \frac{2\pi r}{t}$$

Try it

Watch the earlier velocity arrows (faded) and the direction in the readout.

  • A square path. How many times does the direction change in one round?
  • Sixteen sides. The turns get smaller and more frequent.
  • A circle. Is there any moment when the direction stays the same?

What you should see: on the square the arrow points one way along each side and swings round at each corner. With more sides the swings get smaller and more frequent. On the circle the arrow turns smoothly all the time, while the speed stays at 15.71 m/s (2π × 15 ÷ 6). Press Let go at any moment and the object carries on along the tangent.

Worked examples

1. A cycling track

A cyclist rides round a circular track of radius 35 m, completing one round in 44 s. Find her speed. (Take π = 22/7.)

  1. \(v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{t} = \dfrac{2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 35}{44} = \dfrac{220}{44} = \) 5 m/s

2. The second hand of a clock

The second hand of a wall clock is 10 cm long. Find the speed of its tip.

  1. \(r = 10\) cm = 0.1 m, and one round takes \(t = 60\) s.
  2. \(v = \dfrac{2 \times 3.14 \times 0.1}{60} \approx \) 0.0105 m/s (about 1 cm/s)

The tip moves at a steady speed, but its velocity changes direction every moment.

3. A merry-go-round

A child sits 3 m from the centre of a merry-go-round that makes one round every 6 s. Find the child's speed, and the distance and displacement after one full round.

  1. \(v = \dfrac{2 \times 3.14 \times 3}{6} = \) 3.14 m/s
  2. Distance in one round = circumference = 18.84 m
  3. Back at the starting point, so displacement = 0

Common mistakes

  • “Constant speed, so no acceleration.” The direction keeps changing, so the velocity changes. Uniform circular motion is accelerated motion.
  • “It flies straight outwards when released.” It leaves along the tangent, the direction it was moving at that instant.
  • Using the diameter for r. r is the radius. If you are given the diameter, halve it first.
  • Using the wrong time. In v = 2πr/t, t is the time for one complete round.

Quick check

1. In uniform circular motion, which of these stays constant?

The speed is constant, but the direction, and so the velocity, keeps changing.

2. A stone whirled on a string is released. Which way does it move at first?

It carries on in the direction it was moving at that instant: along the tangent.

3. An object goes round a circle of radius 7 m in 11 s. Its speed is (π = 22/7)…

\(v = 2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \div 11 = 44 \div 11 = 4\) m/s.

4. Why is uniform circular motion called accelerated motion?

Acceleration is a change in velocity, and a change in direction is a change in velocity.
