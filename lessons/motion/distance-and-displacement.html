Laws of Motion · Lesson 1

Distance and Displacement

You walk to a friend's house and back home. You walked a long way, yet you ended up exactly where you started. Physics has a separate word for each of those facts.

Download slides (.pptx) 13 slides with teacher notes

What is motion?

An object is in motion when its position changes with respect to its surroundings. To decide, we compare it with a fixed point called the reference point. A tree beside the road is at rest. A bus passing it is in motion.

Motion depends on the reference point. A passenger sitting in the bus is at rest compared with the seat in front, but in motion compared with the tree.

Distance

Distance is the total length of the path an object actually travels. It does not care about direction. Every step adds to it, so it can only grow.

In the simulation, distance is the violet line, the route the walker really took.

Displacement

Displacement is the minimum distance between the starting point and the finishing point, measured in a straight line, together with its direction. It only cares about where you started and where you are now, not the route in between.

In the simulation, displacement is the teal arrow from Start to the walker.

Both are measured in metres (m). Distance has size only, so it is a scalar. Displacement has size and direction, so it is a vector.

Distance or displacement?

DistanceDisplacement
MeaningLength of the actual pathStraight line from start to finish
DirectionNot included (scalar)Included (vector)
Can it be zero while moving?NoYes, after a round trip
Depends on route?YesNo, only on start and end
Which is bigger?Displacement is never larger than distance. They are equal only for a straight walk with no turning back.

Try it

Predict both numbers first, then press the button and check the readout under the map.

  • Two routes to school. Route A and route B start at the same home and end at the same school. Compare both numbers.
  • Walk 70 m in a straight line. Are distance and displacement equal?
  • Walk 70 m, then come back. What happens to the teal arrow on the way back?
  • Run half a lap of a circular track with radius 20 m.
  • Now run the full lap. Where does the arrow end up?

What you should see: route A is 105 m and route B is 119 m, but both give the same displacement, 78.3 m, because they start and end at the same places. The straight walk gives 70 m for both. The round trip gives a distance of 140 m and zero displacement. Half a lap gives 62.8 m of distance but only 40 m of displacement (the diameter). A full lap brings displacement back to zero.

Worked examples

1. Going to the shop

Riya walks 40 m east and then 30 m north to reach a shop. Find the distance she walked and her displacement.

  1. Distance is the whole path: \(40 + 30 = \) 70 m
  2. Displacement is the straight line from start to shop. The two walks form a right angle, so use Pythagoras: \(\sqrt{40^2 + 30^2} = \sqrt{2500} = \) 50 m
  3. Direction: \(\tan\theta = 30/40\), so \(\theta \approx 37^\circ\). Her displacement is 50 m, 37° north of east

2. Round the track

An athlete runs around a circular track of radius 50 m. Find the distance and displacement after half a lap and after one full lap.

  1. Circumference \(= 2\pi r = 2 \times 3.14 \times 50 = 314\) m.
  2. Half a lap: distance \(= 314 / 2 = \) 157 m. The athlete is now directly across the circle, so displacement is the diameter \(= 2r = \) 100 m
  3. Full lap: distance 314 m. The athlete is back at the start, so displacement is 0 m

Common mistakes

  • Giving displacement without a direction. "50 m" is incomplete. Write "50 m north-east" or "50 m, 37° north of east".
  • Adding the two legs to get displacement. 40 m east and 30 m north is not 70 m of displacement. Draw the straight line from start to finish.
  • Thinking zero displacement means no motion. A full lap is plenty of motion. Only the start and end points coincide.

Quick check

1. A car drives 5 km east and then 5 km west. What are its distance and displacement?

The car covered 5 + 5 = 10 km of road, but it ended where it started, so its displacement is zero.

2. Which of these is a vector quantity?

A vector has both size and direction. Displacement needs a direction to be complete; the others do not.

3. Can the displacement of an object ever be larger than the distance it travels?

A straight line is the shortest path between two points, so displacement is at most equal to the distance.

4. A student sitting in a moving train is…

Rest and motion always depend on the reference point you choose.
