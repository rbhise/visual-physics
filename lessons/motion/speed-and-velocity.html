Speed and Velocity

Two cyclists both ride at 5 m/s, one heading north and one heading east. Their speeds are equal. Their velocities are not.

Download slides (.pptx) 13 slides with teacher notes

Speed: how fast

Speed is the distance travelled by an object in unit time. It tells you how fast something moves, but not which way.

$$\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$$

The SI unit is metres per second (m/s). Speed has size only, so like distance it is a scalar.

Real journeys speed up and slow down, so we usually talk about average speed: the total distance divided by the total time taken.

Velocity: how fast, and which way

Velocity is the displacement that occurs in unit time. Because displacement has a direction, so does velocity: it is a vector.

$$\text{velocity} = \frac{\text{displacement}}{\text{time}}$$

The unit is also m/s, but a velocity is only complete with its direction: "5 m/s north", not just "5 m/s". In the simulation, the ink arrow labelled v on the cyclist is the velocity. Its length shows the speed and it points the way the cyclist is moving.

Velocity changes in three ways: when the speed changes, when the direction changes, or when both change. A cyclist going round a circular track at a steady speed still has a changing velocity, because the direction turns all the time.

Changing units: km/h and m/s

Speeds on roads are given in kilometres per hour. Since 1 km = 1000 m and 1 h = 3600 s:

$$1\ \text{km/h} = \frac{1000\ \text{m}}{3600\ \text{s}} = \frac{5}{18}\ \text{m/s}$$

Each button rides at 8 m/s. Watch the arrow labelled v and the averages in the bottom row.

  • Straight road. Does the velocity arrow ever change?
  • There and back. Compare average speed with average velocity at the finish.
  • Circular track. The speed never changes. What does the arrow do?

What you should see: on the straight road the arrow keeps the same length and direction, and average speed equals average velocity. After the round trip, average speed is 8 m/s but average velocity is 0. On the track the arrow keeps its length but swings round, so the velocity changes even though the speed does not.

Worked examples

1. A bus journey

A bus covers 90 km in 1.5 hours. Find its average speed in km/h and in m/s.

  1. Average speed \(= \dfrac{90\ \text{km}}{1.5\ \text{h}} = \) 60 km/h
  2. In m/s: \(60 \times \dfrac{5}{18} = \) 16.7 m/s

2. Back to Riya's walk

In Lesson 1, Riya walked 40 m east and then 30 m north to a shop. The walk took 50 s. Find her average speed and average velocity.

  1. Distance = 70 m, so average speed \(= 70 / 50 = \) 1.4 m/s
  2. Displacement = 50 m, 37° north of east, so average velocity \(= 50 / 50 = \) 1 m/s, 37° north of east

Average velocity is smaller than average speed, because the displacement cuts the corner.

3. Laps of a track

A cyclist rides once around a circular track 200 m long in 40 s. Find the average speed and average velocity for the lap.

  1. Average speed \(= 200 / 40 = \) 5 m/s
  2. She finishes where she started, so displacement = 0 and average velocity = 0 m/s

Common mistakes

  • Mixing units. Don't divide metres by hours. Convert first, using 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s.
  • Using displacement for speed. Speed uses distance, the whole path. Velocity uses displacement.
  • "Constant speed means constant velocity." Only on a straight line. Turning changes the velocity.
  • Leaving out the direction. A velocity without a direction is just a speed.

Quick check

1. An auto-rickshaw goes round a roundabout at a steady 20 km/h. Its velocity is…

Velocity includes direction. Turning changes the direction, so the velocity changes even at a steady speed.

2. A train moves at 54 km/h. What is that in m/s?

\(54 \times 5/18 = 15\) m/s.

3. A runner completes one full lap of a 400 m track in 100 s. Her average velocity is…

She ends where she began, so her displacement is zero. Her average speed is 4 m/s, but her average velocity is 0.

4. Which statement is always true?

Displacement is never more than distance, and both are divided by the same time.
