Valency and Chemical Formulae

Why is water H₂O but table salt NaCl, and why does aluminium oxide need two aluminium atoms for every three oxygen? The answer is each element's combining capacity.

Valency

The valency of an element is its combining capacity: the number of hydrogen atoms (or other atoms of valency 1) that one of its atoms can combine with. It depends on how many electrons the atom gives away, takes in or shares.

  • Hydrogen, sodium, potassium and chlorine have valency 1.
  • Oxygen, magnesium and calcium have valency 2.
  • Aluminium and nitrogen (in ammonia) have valency 3. Carbon usually has valency 4.

Some elements show variable valency. Iron can have valency 2, as in iron(II) chloride FeCl₂, or 3, as in iron(III) chloride FeCl₃. Copper can be 1 or 2.

Radicals

Some groups of atoms stay together and act as a single unit with their own valency. These are radicals (polyatomic ions): ammonium NH₄⁺, hydroxide OH⁻, nitrate NO₃⁻, sulphate SO₄²⁻, carbonate CO₃²⁻ and phosphate PO₄³⁻.

Writing a formula: the criss-cross method

  1. Write the symbols, positive part first: Al and O.
  2. Write each valency above its symbol: Al 3, O 2.
  3. Cross over: the valency of one becomes the subscript of the other: Al₂O₃.
  4. Divide by any common factor to get the simplest ratio: Mg₂O₂ becomes MgO.
  5. Put a radical in brackets if you need more than one of it: Ca(OH)₂, not CaOH₂.

The simulation shows each step. The total positive valency then equals the total negative valency: in Al₂O₃, \(2 \times 3 = 3 \times 2 = 6\).

Try it

Choose the two ions and follow the arrows.

  • Aluminium oxide. Valencies 3 and 2.
  • Magnesium oxide. Both valency 2: simplify.
  • Calcium hydroxide. A radical needs brackets.
  • Ammonium sulphate. Two radicals.

What you should see: aluminium oxide is Al₂O₃ (102 u). Magnesium oxide first gives Mg₂O₂, which simplifies to MgO. Calcium hydroxide is Ca(OH)₂: the brackets show two whole OH groups. Ammonium sulphate is (NH₄)₂SO₄ (132 u).

Worked examples

1. Aluminium oxide

Write the formula of aluminium oxide and find its formula mass. (Al = 27, O = 16)

  1. Valencies: Al 3, O 2. Cross over: Al₂O₃
  2. Mass \(= 2 \times 27 + 3 \times 16 = 54 + 48 = \) 102 u

2. Calcium hydroxide

Write the formula of calcium hydroxide.

  1. Valencies: Ca 2, OH 1. Cross over: Ca₁(OH)₂
  2. Drop the 1 and keep the brackets: Ca(OH)₂, mass \(40 + 2 \times 17 = 74\) u

3. Iron chlorides

Write the formulae of iron(II) chloride and iron(III) chloride.

  1. Iron(II) has valency 2: FeCl₂
  2. Iron(III) has valency 3: FeCl₃

The Roman numeral in the name tells you which valency the iron has.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to simplify. Mg₂O₂ is not a formula; divide by 2 to get MgO.
  • Missing brackets. CaOH₂ would mean one O and two H. Write Ca(OH)₂ for two hydroxide groups.
  • Negative part first. Write the metal or positive ion first: NaCl, not ClNa.
  • Ignoring variable valency. Check the name: iron(III) oxide is Fe₂O₃, iron(II) oxide is FeO.

Quick check

1. The formula of magnesium chloride is…

Mg has valency 2, Cl has valency 1: MgCl₂.

2. What is the valency of oxygen?

Oxygen combines with two hydrogen atoms in H₂O.

3. Which is the correct formula for sodium sulphate?

Na valency 1, SO₄ valency 2: Na₂SO₄.

4. A group of atoms that acts as one unit with its own valency is called a…

Examples are NH₄⁺, OH⁻ and SO₄²⁻.
