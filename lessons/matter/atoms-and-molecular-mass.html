Atoms, Molecules and Molecular Mass

A single atom of carbon weighs about 2 × 10⁻²⁶ kg, far too small for any balance. So chemists compare atoms with each other instead, using a special unit.

Atoms and molecules

An atom is the smallest particle of an element that takes part in a chemical reaction. A molecule is the smallest particle of a substance that can exist independently and still has all the properties of that substance.

  • Some elements exist as single atoms, like helium (He).
  • Many elements exist as molecules of the same atoms: oxygen O₂, hydrogen H₂, nitrogen N₂.
  • Compounds are molecules of different atoms: water H₂O, carbon dioxide CO₂, ammonia NH₃.

The atomic mass unit

Atoms are too light to weigh directly, so their masses are compared with the carbon-12 atom. The unified atomic mass unit (u), also called the dalton (Da), is one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom:

$$1\ \text{u} = \tfrac{1}{12} \times \text{mass of one C-12 atom} \approx 1.66 \times 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}$$

The relative atomic mass of an element tells how many times heavier its atom is than 1 u. Carbon is 12 u, oxygen 16 u, hydrogen about 1 u.

Molecular mass

The molecular mass of a substance is the sum of the atomic masses of all the atoms in one molecule. Multiply each atomic mass by the number of those atoms, then add.

$$\text{H}_2\text{O}: \quad 2 \times 1 + 1 \times 16 = 18\ \text{u}$$

Try it

Pick a molecule and check the sum in the table beside it.

  • Carbon dioxide. One carbon, two oxygen.
  • Sulphuric acid. Seven atoms of three elements.
  • Calcium carbonate. The main part of marble and limestone.
  • Glucose. 24 atoms in one molecule.

What you should see: CO₂ is 12 + 32 = 44 u. H₂SO₄ is 2 + 32 + 64 = 98 u. CaCO₃ is 40 + 12 + 48 = 100 u. Glucose is 72 + 12 + 96 = 180 u. Try building your own too: add atoms one at a time.

Worked examples

1. Ammonia

Find the molecular mass of ammonia, NH₃.

  1. \(1 \times 14 + 3 \times 1 = \) 17 u

2. Sulphuric acid

Find the molecular mass of sulphuric acid, H₂SO₄.

  1. H: \(2 \times 1 = 2\); S: \(1 \times 32 = 32\); O: \(4 \times 16 = 64\)
  2. Total \(= 2 + 32 + 64 = \) 98 u

3. Glucose

Find the molecular mass of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆.

  1. \(6 \times 12 + 12 \times 1 + 6 \times 16 = 72 + 12 + 96 = \) 180 u

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the subscript. In H₂SO₄ there are 4 oxygen atoms: 4 × 16, not 16.
  • Subscripts apply to the next atom only. In CaCO₃, the 3 belongs to O only. Carbon has 1 atom.
  • Writing grams for one molecule. Molecular mass is in u. Grams come in with the mole (next lesson).
  • Mixing up atom and molecule. O is an oxygen atom; O₂ is an oxygen molecule made of two atoms.

Quick check

1. The molecular mass of carbon dioxide (CO₂) is…

12 + 2 × 16 = 44 u.

2. 1 u is defined as…

The unified atomic mass unit is based on carbon-12.

3. How many atoms are in one molecule of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆?

6 + 12 + 6 = 24 atoms.

4. The molecular mass of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) is (Ca = 40)…

40 + 12 + 3 × 16 = 100 u.
