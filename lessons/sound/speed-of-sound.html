The Speed of Sound

At a cricket match you see the bat hit the ball a moment before you hear the crack. Light reaches you almost at once; sound takes its time, and how long depends on what it travels through.

It depends on the medium

Sound is passed on by particles pushing their neighbours. In a solid the particles are tightly linked, so the push passes on very quickly; in a gas they are far apart, so it passes slowly. In general sound travels fastest in solids, slower in liquids and slowest in gases.

MediumApproximate speed of sound
Air at 0 °C332 m/s
Air at 20 °C344 m/s
Waterabout 1500 m/s
Steelabout 5000 m/s

Temperature and density

In a gas, sound travels faster when the gas is warmer: its particles move faster and pass the push along more quickly. For air, the speed rises by about 0.6 m/s for every degree Celsius:

$$v \approx 332 + 0.6\,t \quad (\text{m/s, with } t \text{ in } ^\circ\text{C})$$

At the same temperature, sound is faster in a lighter (less dense) gas. It travels about four times faster in hydrogen than in oxygen. The speed of sound does not depend on its frequency or loudness: every note from an orchestra reaches the back of the hall together.

Light is much faster

Light travels at about \(3 \times 10^8\) m/s, nearly a million times faster than sound in air. In a storm the flash reaches you almost at once, and the thunder, made at the same instant, arrives later. Count the seconds between them and

$$\text{distance} = \text{speed of sound} \times \text{time}$$

What you should see: Steel wins easily (0.2 s), then water (0.67 s), and air comes last (2.91 s at 20 °C). At −10 °C the air speed drops to 326 m/s and the air pulse takes 3.07 s. The thunder from 1720 m arrives after 5.0 s at 20 °C, and after 4.83 s at 40 °C.

Worked examples

1. Warm and cold air

Find the speed of sound in air at 20 °C. At what temperature is it 350 m/s?

  1. \(v = 332 + 0.6 \times 20 = \) 344 m/s
  2. \(350 = 332 + 0.6\,t\), so \(t = 18 \div 0.6 = \) 30 °C

2. How far is the storm?

On a 30 °C evening, Meera hears thunder 3 s after she sees the lightning. How far away was the lightning?

  1. \(v = 332 + 0.6 \times 30 = 350\) m/s
  2. Distance \(= 350 \times 3 = \) 1050 m

3. An ear to the rail

A railway worker taps a steel rail 1000 m away. You have one ear on the rail and the other in the air (20 °C). How far apart are the two sounds?

  1. Through steel: \(1000 \div 5000 = 0.2\) s
  2. Through air: \(1000 \div 344 \approx 2.91\) s
  3. Gap \(\approx\) 2.7 s; you hear the tap twice

Common mistakes

  • “Sound is fastest in air.” It is slowest in gases and fastest in solids.
  • “High notes travel faster.” In one medium, all frequencies travel at the same speed.
  • Ignoring temperature. Air carries sound faster when it is warmer: about 0.6 m/s more per °C.
  • “The thunder happens after the lightning.” They happen together; the sound simply takes longer to arrive.

Quick check

1. In which medium does sound travel fastest?

Sound travels fastest in solids.

2. The speed of sound in air at 25 °C is about…

332 + 0.6 × 25 = 347 m/s.

3. You hear thunder 5 s after the flash. The speed of sound is 340 m/s. How far away is the storm?

340 × 5 = 1700 m.

4. At the same temperature, sound travels faster in…

Sound travels faster in a lighter, less dense gas.
