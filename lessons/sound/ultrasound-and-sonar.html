Study of Sound, lesson 4

Ultrasound and SONAR

Bats fly in pitch darkness, and ships measure oceans thousands of metres deep, using sounds too high for you to hear. The trick is the echo.

What we can hear

The human ear can hear sounds with frequencies from about 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz (20 kHz). This is the audible range, and it shrinks as people grow older.

  • Infrasound: below 20 Hz. Made by earthquakes, volcanoes, whales and elephants. Some animals sense the infrasound of an earthquake before it strikes.
  • Ultrasound: above 20 000 Hz. Bats, dolphins and some insects make and hear it; dogs can hear up to about 45 kHz, which is why a 'silent' dog whistle works.

Why ultrasound is useful

Ultrasound has a very short wavelength. It travels in a narrow beam with little spreading, and it reflects well even from small objects. Its echoes can therefore show where things are and what shape they have.

SONAR

SONAR stands for SOund Navigation And Ranging. A transmitter under a ship sends a pulse of ultrasound downwards. The pulse reflects from the sea bed (or a submarine, a shipwreck or a shoal of fish), and a receiver picks up the echo. If the echo returns after time \(t\) and sound travels at speed \(v\) in sea water,

$$\text{depth} = \frac{v \times t}{2}$$

SONAR is used to measure the depth of the sea, map the sea bed, find submarines and wrecks, and locate shoals of fish.

Ultrasound in medicine and industry

  • Sonography: ultrasound echoes from organs build up a picture of the inside of the body, such as the liver, kidneys or an unborn baby. It uses no harmful radiation.
  • Echocardiography shows the heart beating.
  • Industry: ultrasound finds hidden cracks in metal and cleans small parts such as watch mechanisms.

Try it

Move the ship or press a button.

  • Deep water. Time the echo over the deepest part.
  • Shallow water. How much quicker is the echo?
  • Change the water. Warmer, saltier water carries sound faster.
  • Map it. Let the ship sail and ping all the way.

What you should see: Over the 3000 m trench the echo takes 4.00 s; near the coast (600 m) only 0.80 s. At 1550 m/s the same 3000 m depth gives an echo after 3.87 s, so a ship must know the speed of sound in its water. The mapping run plots a dot for every echo, tracing the sea bed.

Worked examples

1. How deep?

A ship's SONAR receives the echo from the sea bed 4 s after sending the pulse. Sound travels at 1500 m/s in sea water. How deep is the sea?

  1. The pulse goes down and back: total distance \(= 1500 \times 4 = 6000\) m
  2. Depth \(= 6000 \div 2 = \) 3000 m

2. Predicting the echo

The sea is 1800 m deep. How long after a SONAR pulse (1500 m/s) does the echo return?

  1. \(t = 2d / v = 2 \times 1800 / 1500 = \) 2.4 s

3. A bat's squeak

A bat squeaks at 50 000 Hz. Find the wavelength in air (340 m/s). Can you hear it?

  1. \(\lambda = v / \nu = 340 / 50\,000 = \) 0.0068 m = 6.8 mm
  2. 50 000 Hz is above 20 000 Hz: ultrasound, not audible to us

A wavelength of a few millimetres lets the bat detect objects as small as a moth.

Common mistakes

  • Depth = v × t. The pulse goes down and back. Divide by 2.
  • “Ultrasound is very loud sound.” Ultra means beyond: its frequency is above our hearing, whatever its loudness.
  • Using 340 m/s under water. Sound is much faster in sea water, about 1500 m/s.
  • “Infrasound is ultrasound.” Infrasound is below 20 Hz; ultrasound is above 20 000 Hz.

Quick check

1. The audible range of a healthy young person is about…

We hear from about 20 Hz to 20 kHz.

2. A SONAR echo returns after 2 s. Sound travels at 1500 m/s in the sea. The depth is…

Depth = 1500 × 2 ÷ 2 = 1500 m.

3. Which of these produces infrasound?

Elephants communicate with infrasound below 20 Hz.

4. Why is ultrasound used to look inside the body?

Ultrasound's short wavelength gives detailed echoes, and it is safe.
